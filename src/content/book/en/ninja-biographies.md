## Mochizuki Chiyome

In September 1561, Mochizuki Moritoki fell in the Battle of Kawanakajima. He had been lord of Mochizuki Castle in Kitasaku in Nagano, and his ancestors included ninja from Kōga. His widow, Chiyome, withdrew to an old house in the village of Nezu, in Chiisagata in Shinshū, Nagano.

According to tradition, she was offered the leadership of a spy ring there: _miko_ (巫女, women serving at a Shintō shrine) who operated in the Kai and Shinano regions. Chiyome gathered orphaned and runaway girls from many places and trained them as _miko_ – and at the same time as _kunoichi_ (くノ一, female ninja), agents for the Takeda family. As _jōnin_ (上忍, head of a ninja Ryū), she assessed each girl’s talents, traits and character in order to use her in the right place.

## Fūma Kotarō

Kotarō came from Sagami and was the fifth-generation _jōnin_ of the Fūma-Ryū. With 200 followers, known as _rappa_ (乱破) or _suppa_ (透波), he fought as a guerrilla band for the Hōjō family of Odawara.

His most famous fight came in March 1581. Takeda Katsuyori, the son of Takeda Shingen, attacked the Hōjō fortress with a large army. The Takeda dug in at Ukishimagahara, and the Hōjō pitched their camp opposite, on the far bank of the Kisegawa River. During the night, Kotarō’s men crossed the river several times, fell on the Takeda camp and threw it into chaos with their ninja tactics.

The battle is recorded in the <cite>Hōjō Godaiki</cite>. By the time of the Tokugawa _bakufu_ (幕府), all that was left of the Fūma group was a band of pirates preying on the Inland Sea.

## Saiga Magoichi

Two thousand guns – an enormous number for the time. That is how many the Saiga group of Kishū had, and their leader was Saiga Magoichi, born with the family name Suzuki. Magoichi was a master of the Tsuda-Ryū and the Saiga-Ryū and skilled with explosives and firearms. He set up his headquarters on the Saiga headland and recruited his men from the _jizamurai_ (地侍), the local samurai around Saiga Castle. The group had many good snipers, and so it enjoyed the support and goodwill of the regional rulers.

Magoichi fought in the Battle of Naniwa Kanzakigawa, in which Oda Nobunaga, the archenemy of all ninja, also took part. To win it, Magoichi devised _shaki no jutsu_ (<q>throwing away the flags</q>). His men left their own flags behind and moved into their new position under forged flags of Nobunaga. Nobunaga’s troops took them for allies. At the time, such a ruse was considered scandalous and cowardly. But the ninja stood outside the law anyway, so they were free to use any tactic. Magoichi’s strategy combined surprise attacks with highly effective gunfire.

Magoichi was a devout Buddhist. He fought Oda Nobunaga, the oppressor of Buddhism, until his death.

## Suginobō Myōsan

He was born Tsuda Minsan Kanmotsu and took the monk’s name Suginobō Myōsan. Legends make him the founder of the Negoro-Ryū and a gifted firearms expert.

Myōsan’s brother, too, is a well-known figure in the history of firearms, which reached Japan in the mid-16th century. Myōsan heard rumors of a firearms demonstration on Tanegashima and traveled there. He was shown a _koshizashi_ (<q>hip-held cannon</q>), which he took to a blacksmith to have copied. From then on, the _sōhei_ (僧兵, Buddhist warrior monks) took pride in their marksmanship and were known as accurate shots.

The year before, the Negoro group had defeated Toyotomi Hideyoshi. In March 1585 he attacked the Negoro Temple with 25,000 men in return. Myōsan fought bravely, but he came up against Mashita Nagamori, who defeated and killed him.

## Sugitani Zenjūbō

On May 19, 1570, Sugitani Zenjūbō lay in wait to kill Oda Nobunaga with two bullets. Both hit only his shoulder pad; the cruel general was unhurt. Zenjūbō was the eldest son of Sugitani Yototsugu, the _jōnin_ of the 53 ninja families of Kōga. He was a gifted shot, and Rokkaku Yoshikata had recruited him as a sniper against Nobunaga.

After the attempt, Zenjūbō fled into the mountains of Ōmi Province and went into hiding. For four years he escaped his enemy, but in the end he was caught. Nobunaga had him tortured until he confessed to the assassination attempt and revealed everything. Then the Kōga ninja was tortured to death over six days.

## Katō Danzō

Katō Danzō was probably a ninja of the Iga-Ryū; his name appears in the book <cite>Ōmi Yochi Shiryaku</cite> (<q>Gazetteer of Ōmi Province</q>). According to a well-known theory, he was born in what is now Ibaraki Prefecture. As a ninja he went by the nickname Tobi Katō, <q>Leaping Katō</q>, because he was thought to be a master of the ninjutsu jumping techniques.

Danzō wanted a high position in the army of the warlord Uesugi Kenshin. He appeared before Kenshin and demonstrated his _genjutsu_ (幻術, <q>art of illusion</q>). Kenshin wanted to test him further. According to legend, Danzō then made himself invisible, slipped into the castle of Kenshin’s best general and made off with a valuable scroll and a maid. Uesugi still would not hire him. Danzō then offered his services to the rival Takeda Shingen, but Takeda refused as well: he suspected Danzō of being Uesugi’s double agent. Takeda is said to have ordered his retainer Tsuchiya Heihachiro to kill Danzō and so solve the problem.

## Kido Yazaemon

In 1581, Kido Yazaemon, an Iga-Ryū ninja and firearms expert, secretly tried to assassinate Oda Nobunaga, the enemy of all ninja. The attempt failed. Yet it was recorded: in the book <cite>Iranki</cite> about the conquest of Iga.

## Igasaki Dōjun

When Dōjun was born and when he died is uncertain. What is known is that he came from Tateoka in Iga Province, which is why people called him Tateoka no Dōjun. He is said to have founded the 49 ninjutsu Ryū of Iga, and his stories are recorded in the first volume of the <cite>Bansenshūkai</cite>.

Rokkaku Yoshikata of Sasaki in the Ōmi region enlisted Dōjun’s help against Dodo, who had betrayed him. Dōjun brought 44 ninja from Iga and four from Kōga and won Rokkaku the victory. To get into the fortress, Dōjun and his men are said to have used paper lanterns painted with the enemy’s family crest, along with _bakemono jutsu_ (<q>spirit arts</q>).

## Kumawaka

Kumawaka (<q>young bear</q>) came from Chiisagata in Shinshū and is said to have been a _genin_ (下忍, executing ninja) of the Kōga-Ryū, allied with Takeda Shingen. The Takeda family had a force of 70 _suppa_, ninja guerrillas. Thirty of them were assigned in groups of ten to Takeda’s three generals, which let them gather intelligence more effectively in their target area.

In the Battle of Wariga Toge (1561), Kumawaka’s general, Idomi Toramasa, divided his army in two – and then realized that he had left his battle banners behind. Kumawaka was a master of the ninjutsu techniques of sneaking and running. He ran all the way back to his general’s fortress and fetched the flags, in just four hours. Although the guards did not know him as one of their own, he got the flags out of the castle. During the battle, Shingen’s precious book, the <cite>Kokin Wakashū</cite>, was stolen, and suspicion fell on Kumawaka. He found the real thief and returned the book to Takeda.

## Ishikawa Goemon

Neither the Iga nor the Kōga families would have called Ishikawa Goemon one of their own, because he used ninjutsu to steal for himself. Still, the list of historical ninja would be incomplete without him. No one knows for certain where he was born. Three well-known theories name Hamamatsu in Enshū, Shirakawa in Ōshū and the village of Ishikawa in Iga Province.

According to tradition, he started out as a _genin_ of the Iga-Ryū. On October 8, 1594, he was boiled to death in oil. His name does not appear in the <cite>Bansenshūkai</cite>, but in novels and on the stage he often appears as the greatest thief of all time. Today Goemon is the hero of countless manga and films, admired by many children and young people.
