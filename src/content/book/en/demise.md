## Tokugawa Unifies Japan

The last surviving ninja had withdrawn even deeper into the forests and mountains or fled to other clans throughout Japan. Then, in 1582, Oda Nobunaga was assassinated at Honnō-ji.

His ally Tokugawa Ieyasu had to get from Sakai, near Ōsaka, to his castle at Okazaki, near Nagoya. The area around Honnō-ji was too dangerous, which left only one route: through the mountains of Iga and Kōga. Ninja under Hattori Hanzō brought him through safely.

Together with ninja from Kōga, they aided Tokugawa, who became shōgun in 1603 and whose clan controlled Japan for the next 250 years. Some families stood by him because they rejoiced at Nobunaga’s death, others because they saw in Tokugawa a peaceful future. The ninja also helped him destroy the last daimyō who opposed Tokugawa rule.

The irony: what finally ended the ninja was peace, not a battle.

## Ninja as Gardeners and State Police

In a Japan where peace and order had returned, nobody needed ninja anymore. Many _genin_ (下忍) and _chūnin_ (中忍) carried on working on their own, without the philosophical guidance of the _jōnin_ (上忍).

Some found a living with the state. The secret police of the _bakufu_ (幕府) took in many ninja, who now kept watch on the imperial court and kept the daimyō in check. They answered to the _ōmetsuke_ (大目付), _bakufu_ officials of the state police. Others joined the military.

Many turned to crime. Former ninja could often be hired in those days, but whoever hired one got nothing more than a thief or murderer in ninja clothing.

Rulers often took them on as gardeners or servants. The once deadly lone fighters sank to the level of security guards on miserable pay. Before long their work came down to opening doors – and serving as targets when young girls threw snowballs.

The few families who had stayed in the mountains outside Kyōto kept themselves hidden from the Tokugawa.

## The Last Missions of Ninja Warriors

Only twice more were ninja needed. The first time was during the Shimabara Rebellion (1637–1638), when Christians rose up against religious and economic oppression.

The last mission of the Tokugawa ninja came in 1853. The ninja Sawamura Yasusuke was ordered to sneak aboard the ships of Commodore Perry. There he was to search for information that would reveal the true intentions of the foreign barbarians.

The archives of the Sawamura family in Iga Ueno still hold two letters. They contain a Dutch song about the merits of French women in bed and British women in the kitchen.
