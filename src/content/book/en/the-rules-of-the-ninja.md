## Where the Rules Come From

The nine traditions of ninjutsu are said to have survived for more than a thousand years. In his book <cite>Essence of Ninjutsu</cite>, Hatsumi Masaaki traces this to one thing: each _sōke_ (宗家, <q>head of the family</q>) passed on their experience in writing and by word of mouth. These family heads risked their lives to attain enlightenment, and their experience became the rules of the ninja.

The ninja created these rules to attain divine wings. The rules arose naturally, which is why they fit any time and any situation. Through them, training in _ninpō taijutsu_ (忍法体術) makes you aware of _kyojitsu tenkan hō_ (虚実転換法), the proper use of truth and falsehood.

Hatsumi compares the rules to the <q>seven ways, three directions</q> of the ninja method of disguise: seven secrets of disguise and three directions add up to ten. And ten stands for a good force that conquers evil.

## The Ten Rules

### 1. Scout and Protect

With ninjutsu, the ninja infiltrates the enemy camp and observes the situation. From that, strategies for a surprise attack or for unrest inside the enemy’s ranks can be developed. When the enemy stands against us, there is no choice but to strive for victory. Normally, though, you should extinguish your own self to bring your actions to a successful end.

Enemy land here means something with a solid form. Hatsumi points out, however, that enemies can also be found in nature and in the souls of other people. All these efforts serve the rulers and the country: they should protect your own country and yourself and secure peace for both sides.

### 2. Justice, Not Self-Interest

The ninja helps the rulers and the country with a righteous spirit and cultivates the soul for teachers and parents. Using ninjutsu for personal profit, personal desires, or entertainment is forbidden. Anyone who fights for justice has to keep one thing in mind: the enemy’s justice is often wrongly taken for no justice at all.

You should offer your services to your teachers and parents. A Japanese rule says that the bond between parent and child lasts one generation, between husband and wife two, and between teacher and student three. This shows how deeply you are in your teachers’ debt. Hatsumi also speaks of himself here: fifteen years after the death of Takamatsu Toshitsugu, his students have developed so far that he can now call them students.

Whoever uses ninjutsu for entertainment or to perform magic tricks is no longer practicing ninjutsu, but sorcery or witchcraft. Such a person cannot live in a society where people open up to one another. They live with snakes, toads, and snails in a world where the sun never shines.

A mind driven by self-interest and selfish desire sends you in a direction without a goal. Hatsumi compares it to a tram: whoever forgets the rules of the ninja and jumps aboard with violent thoughts will arrive at the terminus <q>criminal</q>, <q>prison</q>, or <q>steel coffin</q>.

Ninjutsu has a peculiar side to it: a bad ninja who profits from what he has learned comes across as a common merchant or a stupid criminal. And just as followers grow lazy once a training hall is firmly established, a ninja’s skills fade when he openly pursues his own needs.

### 3. Truth and Lies

Ninjutsu places great weight on _hojutsu_, which Hatsumi equates with _kyojitsu tenkan hō_: the method of switching back and forth between truth and lies. The ninja can use _taijutsu_ (体術) without revealing himself to the enemy. If there is no other choice, he turns to _happō hiken_ (八法秘剣), the eight methods of handling a sword, or to ninja tools to confuse the enemy.

This confusion is what matters. _Hojutsu_ attacks the mind more than the body. The ninja uses investigation, espionage, and stealth, and places truth and lies close side by side to reach his goal.

### 4. Explosives, Tools, and Drugs

Ninja must master explosives, ninja tools, and drugs, good medicine as well as poisons. At the same time, the rule not to kill an enemy applies. The ninja had recognized that a bad end would destroy them. A person is dead when the spirit is dead, even if the body still lives. The ninja advanced this spirit of justice.

### 5. Mastering Every Weapon

A ninja spends a great deal of time practicing with weapons. It is written that he should take advantage of this and learn from his instincts. The use of weapons changes over time, and what holds today may no longer hold tomorrow. That is why you should practice until you can handle any weapon.

Hatsumi tells of a visit to New York. Everyone was amazed that he could use any weapon put into his hands, and they called him a magician.

### 6. The Language of Nature

A ninja always seeks direct contact with meteorology, physiography, and geography. Through meteorology and physiography, he understands the changes and the true appearance of nature. This is not academic learning. It is an experience that involves the whole body: through the transmission of feelings and sensations, you come to understand the silent language of nature. You could call it <q>talking with God</q>.

### 7. Avoiding the Fight

During Japan’s civil war, ninja who broke the laws were severely punished. Even their closest relatives were beheaded, exiled, or banished. The more the war escalated, the more often ninja found themselves in the tragic position of having to live within the limits of these rules. From this came their philosophy: always try to prevent a fight, and slip away or flee until it is over.

### 8. No Killing, No Stealing

Ninja may not kill anyone, injure honorable people, or steal money or valuables.

### 9. Body and Versatility

A ninja always takes care of himself. He develops a strong body and quick movements, and learns and masters as many things as possible. Hatsumi himself loves music, plays electric guitar, performs Japanese dance, enjoys painting and writing, and works hard to develop the soul of ninjutsu.

### 10. Keep Training

Ninja never stop training. The training covers eighteen areas:

- mental development
- unarmed combat
- sword fighting
- staff and stick fighting
- blade throwing
- spear fighting
- sword spear
- chain and sickle
- fire and explosives
- disguise and impersonation
- stealth and methods of breaking in
- horseback riding
- water training
- strategy
- espionage
- escape and concealment
- meteorology
- landscape studies
