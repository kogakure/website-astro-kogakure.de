## Ryū

The public knows little about true ninjutsu. Many take it for a martial sport like karate or jūdō. It is not. Ninjutsu, or _ninpō_ (忍法), is the umbrella term for a range of styles, schools, and views. Each of these Ryū (流) had its own techniques, wisdom, and specialties. During the Sengoku jidai (戦国時代) there were probably around 250 Ryū. Some had their own fighting style, but often several Ryū shared the same or similar styles.

By the end of the war, most Ryū had disappeared. Some had been nothing more than hired mercenaries, assassins, or spies.

This side of history should be accepted. Ninja teachers and historians often insist that ninja were only scouts. That is certainly wrong. As today, _in_ (陰) and _yō_ (陽) were in balance back then. There were unscrupulous people who took any job for money, and there were Ryū under the wise leadership of a _jōnin_ (上忍). In times of war a Ryū had to be led with philosophical foresight, because a friend could turn into an enemy in a single breath.

Students of the Bujinkan do not identify with the crimes of the past. They model themselves on the ideals of the teachings that stood at the beginning of the Ryū: honesty, humility, humor, strength, curiosity, self-control, understanding, and altruism. A ninja always lives by the rule that happiness, joy, and personal fulfillment are the birthright of every human being, regardless of skin color, religion, or beliefs.

Their task is to preserve these ideals and to watch over them. They devote their whole lives to keeping the peace in word and deed. In the last resort this can mean having to fight. But a true ninja first tries the path of nonviolence.

## The Nine Schools

Under Dr. Hatsumi Masaaki, nine warrior and ninja schools were brought together that had been passed down from teacher to student for generations. In honor of his teacher Takamatsu Toshitsugu, Hatsumi named these nine _ryūha_ (流派) the Bujinkan (武神館). _Bu_ means war, _jin_ means god, heart, or soul, and _kan_ means palace, building, or great house. One possible translation is therefore <q>the hall of the god of war</q>.

According to their own tradition, the oldest of these schools look back on a good thousand years. Their techniques have been used often enough over time to test and prove that they work. Ninjutsu is not a Zen system. The goal is not the technique but solely to defeat the opponent quickly and effectively. There are no showy techniques and no wasted movements. Ninjutsu is effective and has no need to measure itself against any other fighting style.

The nine _ryūha_ of the Bujinkan are:

1. Togakure-Ryū (戸隠流) _ninpō_
2. Gyokko-Ryū (玉虎流) _koshi jutsu_ (骨指術)
3. Kukishinden-Ryū (九鬼神伝流) _happō hikenjutsu_ (八法秘剣術)
4. Shinden-Fudō-Ryū (神伝不動流) _daken taijutsu_ (打拳体術)
5. Gyokushin-Ryū (玉心流) _ninpō_
6. Kotō-Ryū (虎倒流) _koppō jutsu_ (骨法術)
7. Takagi-Yōshin-Ryū (高木楊心流) _jū tai jutsu_ (柔体術)
8. Gikan-Ryū (義鑑流) _koppō jutsu_
9. Kumogakure-Ryū (雲隠流) _ninpō_
