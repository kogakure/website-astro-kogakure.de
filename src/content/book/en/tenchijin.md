A ninja can turn a situation in their favor in three ways. They can change the environment and stay as they are. They can change themselves while the environment stays the same. Or they can change nothing at all and only make the situation look different. These three ways are called _ten_ (天, <q>heaven</q>), _chi_ (地, <q>earth</q>), and _jin_ (人, <q>human</q>). Behind them stands the Daoist view of the world: each time, the ninja shifts the balance between _in_ (陰) and _yō_ (陽) and looks at the situation from a different angle.

## Ten or Heavenly Principles

An opponent who ought to rest is itching for a fight. Another who wants to attack is forced to wait. Both are heavenly principles. The ninja alters the world around them by changing their surroundings, shifting the balance between _in_ and _yō_ so that they can remain the same while the environment has to change.

Raising the influence of _yō_ on the environment makes the opponent more vulnerable. You can make them eager to fight when rest would serve them better. You can keep them moving when they should be resting. Or you can lead them to give up a good position of their own accord.

Raising the influence of _in_ means making the eager opponent wait. Nothing is more frustrating than holding back surging energy and biding your time. You can also leave them unsure whether their information is correct, or lure them into narrow corridors and rooms where they cannot bring their superior numbers to bear.

### Gojō Goyoku

The tactic of _gojō goyoku_ (五情五欲) starts from a person’s weak points. The ninja influences others by using their own weaknesses and mistakes to weaken and steer them. It has five principles:

- **_Kisha_** targets vanity. The ninja flatters and praises the opponent until vanity brings them down.
- **_Dosha_** exploits a hot temper and lack of self-control. The opponent is provoked into rash, foolish acts.
- **_Aisha_** exploits generosity and sentimentality. The opponent is made to feel pity or regret.
- **_Rakusha_** exploits laziness and inertia. Money, luxuries, women, or comfort bring the opponent down.
- **_Kyōsha_** exploits cowardice and fear. Threats, intimidation, and blackmail keep the opponent under control.

Each of these tactics works only for a limited time; sooner or later it wears out. The ninja must therefore weigh carefully whether _gojō goyoku_ pays off in the long run.

Another route to influence runs through a person’s wants and needs: whoever satisfies them gains power over that person. Every human being is subject to five great needs. There are others, but most fit into one of these five categories.

#### Safety

People on the run need shelter and food. By offering both, the ninja makes them dependent and gains control over them. Ideals and political convictions fade as soon as survival is no longer secure.

#### Sex

Almost everyone depends on having their sexual desires fulfilled. Men in particular are susceptible to this and therefore easy to control. When love enters the picture, caution and common sense fall by the wayside.

#### Wealth

<q>Every man has his price.</q> Everyone is after a certain measure of wealth, which in turn usually meets their other needs as well. With the right bribe, anyone can be influenced and controlled. That way the ninja secures their loyalty, if only for a while.

#### Pride

Everyone needs recognition for what they achieve, and from this grows pride. When a ninja seems to raise another person up and showers them with pride, that person becomes dependent and open to manipulation and control.

#### Pleasure

Most people need pleasure, joy, and distraction. It is no accident that television, games, and events are always in demand. When a ninja provides someone with pleasure, that person becomes dependent on the ninja and is easy to control.

## Chi or Earth Principles

With the earth principles, the environment stays as it is. Instead, the ninja changes, and in doing so creates new conditions.

Raising the influence of _yō_ on yourself creates more favorable conditions. If the opponent is stronger or better, you practice and improve until you can outdo them. If they have more information, you gather more of your own. If their troops outnumber yours, you strengthen your own ranks.

Raising the influence of _in_ on yourself reduces the danger. When pursued, the ninja drops back until they are behind the enemy. When they fall into a raging torrent, they don’t fight the water but let themselves drift downstream. When exposed as an agent, they offer their services to the enemy.

This also means not judging a situation too quickly, but assessing it neutrally. A murderer lies in wait in a dark house. You picture him waiting in the darkness, weapon drawn, knowing exactly where you are. Change your point of view, and the same murderer suddenly seems small and forlorn. He is waiting in the dark, not knowing whether you will even enter the house, which way you will go, or how you are armed. He feels lost in the darkness.

The same goes for a large, physically superior opponent. A single blow from him, it seems, could destroy you. From another angle, he looks immobile, hampered by his bulk and muscles. Speed lets you hit him in vulnerable spots he can barely protect.

## Jin or Human Principles

In the Middle Ages, a ninja could run screaming wildly toward the guards at a castle gate and report an attack on the far side of the castle, or a fire. The guards rushed off in that direction, and the ninja left the castle unhindered.

Or the ninja threw a heavy stone from the wall into the water. The guards now concentrated on searching the bank, and the ninja escaped somewhere else.

These are human principles. Neither the environment nor the ninja changes. Illusions and tricks merely stage a changed situation. To do this, the ninja exaggerates _in_ and _yō_ so far that an outsider can no longer see how things really stand.

This tactic is called _kyojitsu tenkan hō_ (虚実転換法, <q>lie and truth</q>). The ninja passes off the truth as a lie and the lie as the truth. They show strength when they are weak and hide their strength when they are strong. They can lay it on so thick that the opponent senses a deception where there is none.

One technique works especially well in close combat. If the opponent tries to grab you, you don’t resist but pull them even closer. By reflex, they shift their force the other way and pull back with all their strength. That is the moment to turn their force around and throw them.

If an attacker is chasing you, you can stop short, trip them, and flee in another direction. Or you can leap at the opponent, lock your legs behind their back, drop to the ground, and pull them down with you.

A ninja did not shy away from dirty tricks. In a fight to the death, it makes no difference how honorably you died. Pulling hair, flattening the nose, tearing at the ears, kicking the groin, throwing sand in the eyes: these are only a small part of the repertoire.

An important principle of ninjutsu is _in shin ton kei_, the greatest effect with the least effort. The ninja always tried to change the natural course of things as little as possible.
