## Strengthening the Body Against Attacks

An untrained body weapon quickly swells up painfully or bruises. That is why hardening is essential. The _daken taijutsu_ (打拳体術) of ninjutsu includes two methods that, according to tradition, were developed in China: _koppō jutsu_ (骨法術), with techniques that break the opponent’s harder bones, and _koshi jutsu_ (骨指術), with thrusts against muscles and organs. To make the body weapons withstand these blows, there are many ways to harden them.

## Fingers and Hands

For the fingers and hands, ninja take the cat as a model. They strike their hands or fingers against a tree and gradually strengthen them. A cushion filled with lead shot or stones works as well. You can strike the cushion or grab into it with force.

The best way to harden the spear hand is to drive it into a bucket filled with sand, lead shot, or pebbles. You start with soft sand. As you progress, harder sand follows, then small stones, then larger ones.

## Arms and Shins

The best way to strengthen the arms and shins is to strike them against a tree. Over time, an opponent’s blows to these <q>sensitive</q> spots lose their effect. The abdomen, solar plexus, neck, and other sensitive areas, on the other hand, are better protected by muscle training.

## Hardening Is Uncomfortable

Hardening may be uncomfortable, but it should not be painful. The body needs enough rest between training sessions. Only a slow increase leads to success.

The other body weapons are also strengthened on trees or cushions. With enough stamina, they become effective tools. Grandmaster Takamatsu is said to have torn whole pieces of bark from a tree with his fingers. His fingernails and toenails, by contrast, are documented: photos show that after years of training they were as sharp and hard as daggers.
