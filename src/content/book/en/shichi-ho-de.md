## Vanishing in the Flood of Stimuli

A flute played alone is easy to hear. In a concert, it disappears into the mass of sound and can only rarely be picked out.

A fine oil painting works the same way. Hanging alone on a wall, it catches the eye. Hanging in a hall among many others, it gets lost.

In everyday life we pass countless people, and most of them we notice only briefly or not at all. The bigger the city, the stronger this effect. In the countryside a stranger may stand out at once; in a big city, they move about unbothered.

The mind filters out everything that is not needed for survival. Only certain people get through that filter: those we know, and those with a striking build, an unusual manner, or distinctive physical features. Everyone else we usually do register, and we may even meet their eyes in passing. But a few moments later most of them have slipped out of our awareness again.

For the ninja of the Middle Ages, this meant spending most of a mission in an inconspicuous disguise. Only a very small part of their missions was carried out in the typical black combat suit.

## The Seven Disguises in the Middle Ages

The medieval system of **_shichi hō de_** (七方出) consisted of seven disguises, seven occupations that were common at the time. It was a way of thinking, not a limit. Ninja always adapted their disguise to the surroundings and the purpose, and also appeared as doctors, travelers, farmers, soldiers, fishermen, or in other roles.

- _akindo_ (商人), merchant
- _hōkashi_, musician
- _komusō_ (虚無僧), wandering priest
- _rōnin_ (浪人), masterless samurai
- _sarugaku_, entertainer
- _shukke_, Buddhist monk
- _yamabushi_ (山伏), warrior mountain ascetic

Two arts make up _shichi hō de_. _Hensō jutsu_ (変装術, <q>the art of disguise</q>) taught the ninja to put on a disguise and fit it to their own body. _Gisō jutsu_ (擬装術, <q>the art of thinking oneself into others</q>) taught them to imitate a person: which traits and qualities they had to make their own.

## A Modern Adaptation

Walking unnoticed through an area of operations as a samurai is no longer possible today. But the methods of ninjutsu are timeless; they just need adjusting from time to time. Stephen K. Hayes developed an up-to-date version of _shichi hō de_ with seven groups.

### Scholars

Students, teachers, scientists, technical specialists, artists, and idealists. In this role you know the university, school, or institution you supposedly belong to, and you usually master a field of your own. An artist must be able to show work and prove their talent; an idealist must identify with the cause they stand for.

### Business People

Salespeople, traders, office workers, secretaries, accountants, and business owners. You need a basic grasp of your field and the bearing of a businessperson; experience and knowledge of the industry must come through. Business cards, annual reports, and other papers are forged.

### Country People

Farmers, ranchers, foresters, lumberjacks, migrant workers, and everyone else you would normally meet in the countryside. Playing one of them means knowing about cows, farming, or forestry, handling tractors, farm machinery, or hunting weapons, and carrying papers such as a hunting or fishing license. This is probably the hardest role: in the countryside a stranger stands out at once and is watched closely.

### Clergy

Priests, rabbis, preachers, missionaries, sect leaders, and possibly social workers. Anyone posing as one of them must know the rules, regulations, and taboos of the religion in detail. Rules of prayer, dietary laws, and certain ways of behaving must be strictly observed.

### Public Figures

Entertainers, actors, musicians, top athletes, politicians, reporters, models, and other figures from the tabloid press. What counts is a certain skill in the field and the right manner, plus autograph cards, photos, party documents, and the like.

### Laborers and Craftspeople

Everyone who works with their body: construction workers, painters, gardeners, truck drivers, plumbers. Tools and know-how are part of the role. Keep in mind, too, that physical work may show on the body.

### Uniformed Services

Repair crews, water and power company staff, prison guards, police officers, nurses, soldiers, security officers, and guards. This cover probably gets you the furthest, possibly even into restricted areas. The necessary papers and permits go without saying.

These groups are far from exhausting the possibilities. A modern ninja is held back by no limit and gives free rein to their imagination.

## Adapting to the Disguise

The right role is not the one that is easiest to play but the one that brings the ninja closest to the goal. Getting there can take effort. The ninja may first have to fit in with a crew of workers for a while before reaching the target location or person.

### Type

The role must suit your type. A small, slim person with soft skin cannot play a construction worker without taking a risk. Nobody believes a young person is a company boss or a dignitary, and nobody believes an older person is a student.

### Hair and Body Shape

Hairstyle and hair color are the easiest things to change. Changing your body shape is far harder and takes a lot of time or elaborate costuming. Posture, gait, and manners can be adapted through persistent practice; disguise, makeup, and costume are easy to adjust.

### Knowledge and Skills

Acquiring the specialist knowledge of an occupation is much harder for a ninja. Books, universities, and courses prepare you for a profession. But you may be forced to prove what you know, and depending on the profession, that can be more or less difficult.

### Language

Language is another hard part of the transformation. An experienced ninja speaks as little as possible to avoid giving themselves away. In some professions too much reserve looks suspicious. Silence is still usually safer, because it keeps you out of any back-and-forth of questions and answers. Pronunciation, register, and accent must be right; jargon, idioms, and slang must fit the role exactly. Abroad there is the added barrier of a foreign language: if you cannot speak without an accent, you stand out as a foreigner at once.

### Area of Operations

It is just as important to know the geography of the area of operations. The ninja knows the escape routes and can find their way around the workplace.

### Psyche

Taking on another person’s role means taking on their psychology too. You may have to say things you find repugnant or behave in ways you normally detest. Without convincingly acted conviction, the role will not be convincing enough.

Remember, too, that most people keep their work and private lives apart. Police officers don’t talk about crime and fighting it all the time.

In _shichi hō de_, the ninja turns their whole mind to the role and for a time becomes another person. But they must not lose themselves in it so completely that they can no longer return to their old _self_, or no longer want to. That takes a remarkably strong personality.
