## The Eight Gates of the Ninja

A ninja Ryū must teach eight fields to truly be a ninja art. This definition is called the _ninja no hachimon_, the eight gates of the ninja.

<img src="/images/article/ninja-no-hachimon-01-eight-gates-of-training.webp" alt="In front of a gate in a castle wall, three hooded ninja train: one kindles a straw fire on the ground, one thrusts with a spear, and one throws blades at a wooden post.">
<figcaption>The eight gates of training</figcaption>
</figure>

1. _Ninja no kiai jutsu_: energy discharge
2. _Ninja no taijutsu_: body art
3. _Ninja no ken_: sword fighting
4. _Ninja no yari_: spear fighting
5. _Shurikenjutsu_: blade throwing
6. _Ka jutsu_ (火術): fire, smoke, and explosives
7. _Kyomon_: observation, common sense
8. _Yūgei_: social arts such as the tea ceremony or ikebana

### Ninja Ryū and Warrior Ryū

It follows that many arts that claim to be ninja arts are in fact warrior Ryū. In the **Bujinkan** (武神館), three _ryūha_ (流派) are pure ninja Ryū: the **Togakure-Ryū** (戸隠流) Ninpō, the **Gyokushin-Ryū** (玉心流) Ninpō, and the **Kumogakure-Ryū** (雲隠流) Ninpō.

The other six _ryūha_ are warrior Ryū. That does not make them any less effective. Even in earlier times, ninja learned other _ryūha_ to improve their skills.

Many famous ninja, such as Hattori Hanzō, were respected warriors in their region. Only a few people knew that they were also ninja.
