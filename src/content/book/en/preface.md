This online book only exists because two things came together in 1999: my interest in ninjutsu and my interest in web design.

My first encounter with ninja was probably the Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles. The first ninja book I discovered was in a local bookstore: Andrew Adams’s <cite>Ninja</cite>. After that, I wanted to know more. I read the books of Stephen K. Hayes and Wolfgang Ettig and ordered many more.

In the ’90s, information about ninjutsu was hard to find. There were only a few dōjō in the German-speaking world, and none of them was within my reach. Getting books was a struggle. In the store, you could only check whether a title was in stock. I ordered most books by their cover from a catalog or a list, without ever having held them in my hands. Then I waited weeks or months for them to arrive at the store, waiting for a call or asking every week whether the book had finally come in. Later, at the Tower of London, I bought a book by Stephen Turnbull with illustrations of ninja. Only with the internet and Amazon did it get easier, and at last I could order the books of Hatsumi Masaaki and many others.

I collected every piece of information that fell into my hands and took notes on it. That collection grew into the website you are reading today.

I write Japanese words in Hepburn romanization. The exceptions are a few terms that have long become everyday words, such as ninja, ninjutsu, or samurai. If a term is unclear, you will find it in the glossary with a fuller explanation. I never put Japanese words into the plural, even where that would be allowed, because to me it simply sounds awful. One ninja. Two ninja. Many ninja.

### History

How ninja and ninjutsu came into being, which fighting schools existed, and which were the largest and best known. Also biographies, the conflicts in the ninja heartland, the decline of the ninja, and recent history.

### Organization & Training

How a ninja school was structured and how the ninja were trained.

### Body Training

Physical abilities, fighting stances, breakfalls, sneaking techniques, and related topics.

### Weapons & Tools

The vast arsenal of the ninja: weapons, gear, poisons, medicines, and food.

### Mental Powers

The mental powers of the ninja and their philosophical and religious concepts.

### Strategy & Tactics

The many strategies of espionage and warfare, escape techniques, and the techniques of hiding and disguise.

### The Legacy of the Ninja

The biographies of the grandmasters Takamatsu and Hatsumi, the Bujinkan organization, and the nine schools of the Bujinkan.

### Rules & Philosophy

To close, a few rules and philosophies.
