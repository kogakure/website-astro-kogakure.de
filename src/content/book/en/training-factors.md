Four factors decide whether a fighter wins a fight: endurance, speed, strength, and flexibility.

## Endurance

A skilled fighter has to keep up their performance for the entire fight. That takes endurance: resistance to fatigue under high external and internal loads that last a long time or keep repeating, and the ability to recover quickly.

For long-term endurance, 90 minutes of forest running a week is recommended, which means about half an hour every other day.

### Why Run?

In <cite>The Art of Expressing the Human Body</cite>, Bruce Lee gives a whole list of reasons. The first is time. A day has 1,440 minutes, a week 10,080. Ninety of them spent running is very little. If you are over 30 and unwilling to invest that time, Lee says, be prepared to spend more of it being sick later.

Running is also safe. Your stamina builds gradually, overexerting yourself is hardly possible, and you start at whatever level suits your fitness. Heart, lungs, and circulation grow stronger, the body takes in more oxygen, and running even trains the organs, which no muscle training reaches. One day your life may depend on that fitness.

Your appearance benefits too. Skin and muscles improve, and runners look more optimistic. Fat around the hips and legs melts away, the muscles firm up, the belly flattens. According to Lee, fat deposits turn into muscle, and with a healthy diet, weight loss is certain. Men and women alike can reduce their waistline by running.

Finally, running builds endurance and confidence. Fit people get more done at work and in their free time without worrying about their hearts. But a healthier life only begins with regular practice over a long period. Short bursts of training with long breaks in between achieve little.

For short-term endurance there is a simple drill: two people hold the fighter, who has to break free.

## Speed

In _taijutsu_ (体術), speed is decisive, because it leaves the opponent no time to react. Speed means the ability to move at maximum velocity. With extreme speed and a brief moment of tension, you can even smash things that are harder than your own bones.

There are two kinds of speed:

1. **Reaction speed**: responding to an opponent’s action in a flash. It depends on how fast the cerebrum transmits its commands to the muscles.
2. **Movement speed**: carrying out a movement, such as a throw or a punch, as fast as possible. Here, coordination of the muscles matters most.

## Strength

Strength is the ability to overcome resistance through muscle contraction. There are three kinds: maximum strength, speed strength, and strength endurance.

The right technique, speed, and reaction are the prerequisites for victory. But when two equally matched opponents meet, strength is what counts in the end. A fighter needs maximum strength to lift the opponent and slam them down, strength endurance for locks and holds, and speed strength for punches, kicks, and every kind of body movement, such as jumps.

## Flexibility

A flexible fighter always stays in control of a fight. Flexibility is the ability to perform voluntary movements with a large range of motion in the joints. Even so, it is often neglected: many people lift weights and build muscle mass, and that is exactly what makes mobility suffer.

### Why Stretch?

Bruce Lee gives several reasons for stretching as well. Combined with proper strength training and aerobic training such as running, it covers the last of the three areas that make up complete fitness.

Stretching lowers the risk of injury. Most everyday and sports injuries happen suddenly, through a fall, a car accident, or a collision with another person, or through overstretching of joints, muscles, and connective tissue. The results are muscle pain, dislocations, and sprains. Fighters who stretch regularly have a 50 percent lower risk of such injuries.

Before combat training, stretching improves neuromuscular coordination. Right after training, it prevents sore muscles and speeds recovery. It makes fighters and athletes better: a gymnast with stiff muscles is hard to imagine, and anyone who is flexible automatically has a physical and a psychological advantage.

Done right, stretching is even fun. Everyone knows how good it feels to stretch in bed after waking up. A stretching exercise can in fact be done before you get out of bed, and it prepares the body well for the day.

The exercises of _jūnantaisō_ (柔軟体操) are well suited to improving flexibility.
