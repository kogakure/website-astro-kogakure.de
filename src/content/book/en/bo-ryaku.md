Commands, drumbeats, flag signals: a general in feudal Japan had to master battle strategy (_bōryaku_, 謀略) and battle formations, along with the signals that steered the troops. Every general knew the teachings of Sun Tzu thoroughly. Ninja had to know these strategies too. Only then could they advise their general and see through the enemy’s plans.

## Gankō – Flight of Birds

<figure>
<img src="/images/formations/ganko.webp" alt="Seven warriors in a diagonal line running from upper left to lower right, each one step to the side and one step behind the next, all facing right.">
<figcaption><em>Gankō</em> (雁行)</figcaption>
</figure>

_Gankō_ (雁行) is a flexible arrangement that is easy to change when the need arises. A dense line of archers protects the front and rear, but enough stand on the sides as well in case the enemy changes position. The general stands at the rear with his staff, yet close enough to the center for orders to pass smoothly.

## Hōshi – Arrowhead

<figure>
<img src="/images/formations/hoshi.webp" alt="Ten warriors forming an arrowhead that points right: a long file as the shaft, with two slanting wings spreading back from its tip.">
<figcaption><em>Hōshi</em> (鋒矢)</figcaption>
</figure>

_Hōshi_ (鋒矢) is meant for a heavy attack. A thin line of arquebusiers leads the samurai vanguard. The arquebusiers shoot gaps into the enemy lines, and the samurai drive into them. Because the formation is so mobile, the drummers and flag bearers are placed around the general’s retainers. It is built for fast attacks, so only a few archers and spearmen guard the flanks.

## Hōen – Keyhole

<figure>
<img src="/images/formations/hoen.webp" alt="Twelve warriors standing at even distances on a wide circle with an empty middle, all facing right.">
<figcaption><em>Hōen</em> (方円)</figcaption>
</figure>

Against the arrowhead, _hōen_ (方円, <q>square and circle</q>) is the best defense. Six ranks of arquebusiers, backed by two ranks of archers, stand in front of the hole to receive the arrowhead. The samurai form the shape that gives the formation its name, and it holds firm against such an attack.

## Kakuyoku – Crane Wing

<figure>
<img src="/images/formations/kakuyoku.webp" alt="Seven warriors in a wide V lying on its side, the point to the left and the arms opening to the right.">
<figcaption><em>Kakuyoku</em> (鶴翼)</figcaption>
</figure>

To surround the enemy, _kakuyoku_ (鶴翼) works best. Arquebusiers and archers weaken the enemy’s attack. Then the attackers run into the samurai, who are drawn up like a wing.

## Kōyaku – Ox Yoke

<figure>
<img src="/images/formations/koyaku.webp" alt="Ten warriors in two parallel files of five, running left to right across the field.">
<figcaption><em>Kōyaku</em> (衡軛)</figcaption>
</figure>

_Kōyaku_ (衡軛) intercepts the crane wing, but above all the arrowhead. The second and third companies can quickly form a keyhole and fight the arrowhead effectively.

## Gyorin – Fish Scale

<figure>
<img src="/images/formations/gyorin.webp" alt="Seventeen warriors, among them archers and spearmen, in a blunt wedge pointing right.">
<figcaption><em>Gyorin</em> (魚鱗)</figcaption>
</figure>

_Gyorin_ (魚鱗) is the formation for an army that is outnumbered. At heart it is a blunted arrowhead: not all the strength goes into the attack, and part of it is held back for safety.

## Engetsu – Crescent

<figure>
<img src="/images/formations/engetsu.webp" alt="Twelve warriors in six pairs, shoulder to shoulder, on a half circle that opens downward.">
<figcaption><em>Engetsu</em> (偃月)</figcaption>
</figure>

When the army has its back to the wall, the general turns to _engetsu_ (偃月). The army is not yet surrounded but has already suffered heavy losses. The battered units pull back while the samurai vanguard forms a crescent that adapts easily to the situation.

## Chōda – Long Snake

<figure>
<img src="/images/formations/choda.webp" alt="Eight warriors in one straight file, one behind the other at even distances, running left to right across the field.">
<figcaption><em>Chōda</em> (長蛇)</figcaption>
</figure>

When the army is attacked from two sides at once, _chōda_ (長蛇) is the best choice. The soldiers can quickly turn to face either direction.

These eight formations are only a small selection. There are many others, and many variations of those shown here. The tiger head is used when you want to set up the flight of birds but your own troops are only half as strong. The lying dragon is a variation of the tiger head for fighting on a hill.
