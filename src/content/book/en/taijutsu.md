## Body Training

Ninjutsu was never about winning belts, collecting trophies, or earning titles. This way of fighting was built for survival: to end a fight quickly and effectively, without any self-imposed rules. Its foundation is **_taijutsu_** (体術, <q>body art</q>), the most important basis of ninjutsu.

The training aims at a natural fighting style, shaped entirely to the person who fights with it. Above all, this takes a timeless understanding of how people behave and how the human body is built.

_Taijutsu_ also includes techniques that let weaker people prevail against stronger, faster, or larger opponents.

## Not a Sport, Not a Competition

There is no second place in a street fight. The runner-up may leave in a metal coffin. Even so, many people look down on _taijutsu_, because most still carry ideas of fairness and competitive ethics in their heads. Above all, the Zen-like martial arts that are widespread today, such as karate or jūdō, follow competition rules and fairness. In doing so, they lose their value as self-defense. Ninjutsu, by contrast, aims for victory and uses every means available to get there.

That is why training does not center on drilling techniques by rote. Students learn to become aware of the feelings that arise in a fight. The combat system of ninjutsu has no fixed number of techniques; it consists of countless variations. Unarmed combat mixes with stick techniques, sword cuts, and other weapons, and so students gradually develop a feel for an all-encompassing combat system.

Many other combat systems force their students to respond with fixed sequences of techniques. _Taijutsu_ works the other way around: students learn to shed all the superfluous techniques and movements they have picked up, without noticing, over the years.

## Natural Body Movements

In _taijutsu_ nobody has to imitate animals or bend the body out of its natural posture. Instead, students learn how small changes in stance, or a lower center of gravity, give them a secure, superior footing.

The strength of this combat system rests on the ninja’s trust in harmony with nature. The basic fighting stances and techniques, too, were developed from the elements of the natural world. Five elemental manifestations stand for solid, liquid, energetic, gaseous, and subatomic forces: _chi_ (地, <q>earth</q>), _sui_ (水, <q>water</q>), _ka_ (火, <q>fire</q>), _fū_ (風, <q>wind</q>), and _kū_ (空, <q>void</q>).

- **Earth** stands for stability. You withstand any onslaught and cannot be overcome. The movements come from the hips.
- **Water** stands for evasive movements and sudden, unexpected ones. They come from the abdomen.
- **Fire** stands for determination: quick, dynamic movements from the solar plexus.
- **Wind** stands for defensive fighting in which you cannot be pinned down. The movements come from the area of the heart.
- **The void** is not part of the combat system. It stands for the thoughts, words, and actions that keep a fight from happening at all.
