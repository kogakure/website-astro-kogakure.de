Ninja used the _tōiri_ (遠入, <q>distant entry</q>) tactics in peacetime. They consist mainly of espionage and preventive measures.

## Chikyū Jutsu

A slack bow, biding its time: this was the tactic of a ninja who had been captured. If the enemy lord offered work, the ninja accepted. At the first opportunity, though, the ninja escaped.

## Fukuro Gaeshi Jutsu

After what looked like a quarrel, the ninja parted ways with the old master and changed sides. For a while the ninja served the new master. But when the war reached a decisive phase, the ninja carried out the mission and returned to the old master.

## Hotarubi Jutsu

In this tactic, <q>the glow of a firefly</q> meant sending a messenger through enemy territory with a forged message and letting the enemy intercept it.

## Jōei Jutsu

Here a ninja is sent into enemy territory shortly before hostilities break out. With war preparations under way everywhere, it is much easier to blend in. The ninja can sign on as a laborer or a soldier.

## Katsura Otoko Jutsu

This tactic goes back to an old Chinese tale of the man on the moon. _Katsura otoko_ was the name for a ninja in enemy territory, cut off from comrades and dependent on the help of locals, the _chitsu mishi_. They offered their home as a base of operations, helped with small spying jobs, and quietly kept the ninja supplied.

Sometimes a ninja helped a family as early as peacetime, including with money. In war, the family then owed the ninja a favor.

## Kunoichi Jutsu

Women employed as agents were called _kunoichi_ (くノ一). They could fight just like their male comrades, but most of their training went into courtly manners, refined speech, and psychological influence. Through them it was possible to get close to key men and, at the right moment, send them to the afterlife with poison.

## Minomushi Jutsu

The name of this tactic, _minomushi jutsu_ (身虫術), means <q>worm in the body</q>. The <cite>Bansenshūkai</cite> describes how servants of the enemy were incited to betray their master. A high-ranking person was worth far more than a lowly one. The ninja might even persuade an ally to change sides with all their troops in the middle of a battle.

## Satobito Jutsu

In this tactic, the ninja sought out a discontented subject of the enemy ruler, someone greedy for money or hungry for success, and took that person into service. Such people acted as informants, kept escape routes open, or supplied material and equipment.

## Taniri Jutsu

This tactic was the same as _yamabiko jutsu_, except that a whole team of ninja changed sides.

## Tensui Jutsu

In this tactic (<q>spitting at the sky</q>), an enemy ninja who had been discovered was still fed information. But now it was only harmless information or false reports.

Later, a blatantly false piece of information could make the enemy ninja fall from favor with their master, and perhaps that ninja could even be recruited.

## Yamabiko Jutsu

In this tactic (<q>the echo answering the call</q>), a ninja changed sides and served the new master for years without ever giving cause to doubt their loyalty. At a prearranged sign, such as a light signal or a firework rocket, the ninja changed sides again, but first did enormous damage or carried out another mission.
