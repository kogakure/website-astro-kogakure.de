Up trees, across ravines, into castles: for each of these routes the ninja had ladders, ropes, and special tools.

## Ashi Kō

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/ashi-ko.webp" alt="A forged iron band worn on the foot, with four tapered spikes projecting from its plate beneath the sole.">
<figcaption><em>Ashi kō</em></figcaption>
</figure>

The counterpart to the _shukō_ (手鉤, <q>hand claw</q>) is the _ashi kō_: a device like the one worn on the hand, fastened under the ninja’s foot instead. It made a kick far more damaging, and it made climbing much easier.

## Ippon Sugi Nobori

<figure>
<img src="/images/tools/ippon-sugi-nobori.webp" alt="A short iron cylinder studded with three rings of spikes, a thick hemp rope running through it, both rope ends spliced into loops.">
<figcaption><em>Ippon sugi nobori</em></figcaption>
</figure>

The name means <q>a way to climb a cedar</q>. The ninja climbed trees with it, using the iron spikes to pull themselves higher on the rope bit by bit. In principle it works like today’s tool for climbing telegraph poles.

## Kagi Nawa

<figure>
<img src="/images/tools/kagi-nawa.webp" alt="A four-pronged forged iron grapnel with sharply bent-back hooks beside a coiled hemp rope.">
<figcaption><em>Kagi nawa</em> (鈎縄)</figcaption>
</figure>

The grappling hook _kagi nawa_ (鈎縄) was tied to a rope. Its four prongs caught easily on an obstacle in a tree or on a building.

## Kagi Yari

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/kagi-yari.webp" alt="A long wooden shaft with a straight, double-edged steel point and a curved steel hook branching just below it.">
<figcaption><em>Kagi yari</em> (鈎槍)</figcaption>
</figure>

The ninja of the Togakure-Ryū (戸隠流) often used the hook spear _kagi yari_ (鈎槍). With it they got into trees, over walls, onto ships, even from tree to tree. Hidden on a raised post, a ninja could catch a victim with the hook and haul them up. It also served for fishing, breaking open doors, and carrying luggage.

<img src="/images/tools/hashigo-yari.webp" alt="A long wooden pole with short pegs projecting in pairs along the shaft and a forged iron hook at the top, no spear blade.">
<figcaption><em>Hashigo yari</em></figcaption>
</figure>

## Kasha

<img src="/images/tools/kasha.webp" alt="An open forged-iron frame with a cylindrical roller at the closed end and two long arms ending in small scrolls.">
<figcaption><em>Kasha</em></figcaption>

With this pulley the ninja slid quickly along horizontally stretched ropes.

## Kuda Bashigo

<figure>

<figcaption><em>Kuda bashigo</em> (管梯子)</figcaption>
</figure>

The _kuda bashigo_ (管梯子, <q>pipe ladder</q>) could be folded up very small.

## Kumade

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/kumade.webp" alt="A compact iron claw of four curved prongs meeting in a wrapped ring.">
<figcaption><em>Kumade</em> (熊手)</figcaption>
</figure>

The _kumade_ (熊手, <q>bear claw</q>) is a rake that served for climbing and also as a fearsome weapon.

<figure>
<img src="/images/tools/kumo-bashigo.webp" alt="A single thick hemp rope with wooden sticks pushed through knotted loops as rungs, an iron hook at the top.">

This _kumo bashigo_ (<q>cloud ladder</q>) is a rope with crossbars.

## Kumo Bashigo: Spider Ladder

<figure>
<img src="/images/tools/kumo-gumo-bashigo.webp" alt="Many hemp ropes radiating from a center like a spider web, an iron hook at the end of each, knotted loops along the ropes as footholds.">

The ninja used this _kumo bashigo_ (<q>spider ladder</q>) to climb trees.

## Musubi Bashigo

<figure>
<img src="/images/tools/musubi-bashigo.webp" alt="A single hemp rope with large knotted loops as steps, a double iron grappling hook at one end.">
<figcaption><em>Musubi bashigo</em> (結び梯子)</figcaption>
</figure>

The _musubi bashigo_ (結び梯子, <q>loop ladder</q>) is a rope with loops at set intervals. The ninja put their feet into them, which made climbing easier.

## Musubi Nawa

The _musubi nawa_ (結び縄) is a rope of horsehair. Ropes were often made of hair, women’s hair among them, because it was very tear-resistant.

## Ninja Tō

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/ninja-to.webp" alt="A short sword with a nearly straight blade, an oversized square iron guard, a plain black lacquered scabbard, and a long cord.">
<figcaption><em>Ninja tō</em> (忍者刀)</figcaption>
</figure>

The short sword _ninja tō_ (忍者刀) is said to have been an important tool in the ninja’s kit. It wasn’t pretty or valuable like the samurai’s _tachi_ (太刀) or _katana_ (刀) – it was useful. Its much shorter blade let the ninja move better and fight at close quarters. Sometimes the blade was straight; sometimes the sword was just a shortened _katana_.

The hand guard, the _tsuba_ (鍔), had no ornament. It was usually larger, of plain steel and often square, so it could serve as a climbing aid. The sword was worn at the side and only rarely, when climbing for instance, across the back or the stomach. Poisons, medicines, or chemicals were often hidden in the handle, and the scabbard, the _saya_ (鞘), could be used as a blowgun or a breathing tube.

## Shukō

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/shuko.webp" alt="A forged iron palm band with four tapered spikes, strapped with leather to a round ring around the wrist.">
<figcaption><em>Shukō</em> (手鉤)</figcaption>
</figure>

The _shukō_ is a terrible weapon. Pointed iron spikes sat on a kind of leather band around the arm and turned the ninja’s hand into a tiger’s claw. The spikes tore terrible wounds into an attacker’s face. With the _shukō_ a ninja could also <q>take swords</q>, that is, stop a sword stroke, and climb walls or trees more easily. The counterpart for the feet was called _ashi kō_.

## Taka Bashigo

<figure>
<img src="/images/tools/taka-bashigo.webp" alt="A very long, narrow ladder with round wooden rungs, leaning at an angle, two small hooks at the top.">
<figcaption><em>Taka bashigo</em></figcaption>
</figure>

The ninja crossed chasms with the _taka bashigo_ (<q>high ladder</q>). An extra rope reinforced its middle to keep it from sagging.

## Tobi Bashigo

<figure>
<img src="/images/tools/tobi-bashigo.webp" alt="A long rope ladder with wooden rungs and four large iron hooks, a pair at each end.">
<figcaption><em>Tobi bashigo</em></figcaption>
</figure>

The _tobi bashigo_ (<q>jumping ladder</q>) had hooks at its ends, which made it easier to throw.

## Toki

_Toki_ (登器) is climbing gear.

## Tsuri Bashigo

<figure>
<img src="/images/tools/tsuri-bashigo.webp" alt="A rope ladder of two hemp ropes with wooden rungs, a pair of forged iron hooks at the top and at the bottom.">
<figcaption><em>Tsuri bashigo</em></figcaption>

For safety, the _tsuri bashigo_ (<q>hanging ladder</q>) could be fastened to additional hooks on a wall.

## Yari

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/yari.webp" alt="A straight, double-edged spearhead on a long lacquered shaft with a brass collar.">
<figcaption><em>Yari</em> (槍)</figcaption>
</figure>

Japanese spears, _yari_ (槍), were made for thrusting and were rarely thrown. Their small points slipped easily between the plates of samurai armor. The heads were usually two- or three-sided, and the spear was usually 200 to 350 centimeters long. Oda Nobunaga used _yari_, some of them 640 centimeters long, to protect his _teppō_ (鉄砲) troops from cavalry.

The ninja are said to have often used the _yari_ to vault high over walls or people.
