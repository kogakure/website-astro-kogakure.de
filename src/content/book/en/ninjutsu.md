## What Is Ninjutsu?

Tradition counts Momochi Sandayū among the three great leaders of the Iga-Ryū (伊賀流). Later sources ascribe these words on the purpose of ninjutsu to him:

> Ninjutsu is not intended for the gratification of personal desires. The ninja engages in his art because he is compelled to protect his country, his leaders, or his family.
>
> **Momochi Sandayū**

According to the saying, anyone who practices ninjutsu only to fulfill their own desires will get no benefit from their techniques.

## The Characters

The word ninja cannot be translated directly into any non-Japanese language.

<figure>
<img src="/images/vector/calligraphy/ninja.webp" alt="The two characters 忍者 side by side in black brush calligraphy on pale, fibrous paper.">
<figcaption>Ninja (忍者)</figcaption>
</figure>

The syllable _nin_ (忍), found in both ninja and ninjutsu, can also be read _shinobu_. It combines the meanings <q>endurance</q>, <q>perseverance</q>, and <q>restraint</q>. In the dictionary, _nin_ also stands for <q>stealth</q>, <q>secrecy</q>, or <q>concealment</q>. _Jutsu_ (術) in ninjutsu means <q>art</q>, and _ja_ (者) in ninja means <q>person</q>.

<figure>
<img src="/images/vector/calligraphy/shinobu.webp" alt="The character 忍 written large and centered in black brush calligraphy on pale paper.">
<figcaption><em>Shinobu</em> (忍)</figcaption>
</figure>

The character _nin_ is made up of two other characters. At the top is _yaiba_ (刃), the <q>blade</q>.

<figure>
<img src="/images/vector/calligraphy/yaiba.webp" alt="The character 刃 written large and centered in black brush calligraphy on pale paper.">
<figcaption><em>Yaiba</em> (刃)</figcaption>
</figure>

At the bottom is _kokoro_ (心), the Japanese word for <q>heart</q>.

<figure>
<img src="/images/vector/calligraphy/kokoro.webp" alt="The character 心 written large and centered in black brush calligraphy on pale paper.">
<figcaption><em>Kokoro</em> (心)</figcaption>
</figure>

The structure of the character tells you that the heart, or will, is guided along paths that lead to mastery of the blade as a tool. In a broader sense, it means control over your own body, your soul, and your sense of what is right and what is wrong.

## A Historical Misunderstanding

Few arts have been misunderstood as often as ninjutsu. Historians have to search long and hard for sources that are not exaggerated propaganda, slander, or tall tales.

That is hardly surprising, because the official historians of the time always belonged to the military dictatorship. They therefore mentioned the resistance of the families from the provinces of Iga and Kōga, both regions in central Japan, only with contempt. These families entered the historical record as criminals, insurgents, or assassins.

## History Is Always Subjective

A story always sounds different depending on who wrote it down. Anyone reading history books should keep this in mind.

The case of the ninja can be seen in a similar way to the Christian church. The church, too, rests not on the priestly brutality of the hellish days of the Inquisition in the Middle Ages, but on the Jewish core idea of a carpenter from Israel.

Sadly, little about the ninja has survived for posterity. For many, the ninja will probably always remain the hooded figures who, on moonless nights, in black suits and face masks, with brutal fighting techniques, murderous weapons, and hidden tools, slit their enemies’ throats.

## Ninjutsu and Modern Media

The modern media and the traditionalists of the Asian martial arts have further damaged the reputation of ninjutsu.

In the 1980s, ninjutsu went through a real boom. Videos, comics, and books by self-proclaimed ninja flooded the market. Many climbed from tree to tree through the forests and believed they were **true** ninja.

Hatsumi Masaaki had hoped this wave would pass:

> I hope that after this tide, those few who seek the true essence of ninjutsu will stay behind and help restore the reputation of this ancient art.
>
> **Hatsumi Masaaki**

His hope came true. The media gradually lost interest in the ninja as a subject, and even the <cite>American Fighter</cite> series ended after five installments.

Today, those who want to preserve the true essence of ninjutsu have come together in Hatsumi Masaaki’s **Bujinkan** (武神館, <q>divine warrior</q>) _budō_ (武道, <q>martial art</q>) dōjō (道場, <q>training hall</q>), spread across the whole world.
