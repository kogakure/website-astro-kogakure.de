## 1999

### December 22, 1999

My ninjutsu website went online on December 22, 1999, at first on shared space without its own domain. Later it ran under two different domains before it got its final name: **kogakure** (木隠, <q>hidden behind leaves and trees</q>).

I still remember searching a very thick Japanese dictionary for a term that had a meaning and did not exist on the internet yet.

The design had, <q>of course</q>, animated ninja stars, an unreadable font, and a page counter.

## 2001

### January 2001

The website had started out as a collection of notes. Over the next two years it slowly grew into a larger site, and by January 2001 the work on it was almost complete. By then I had put a downright absurd number of hours into researching and writing the articles. I had also built numerous elaborate 3D models of weapons, tools, and techniques in Poser and 3D Studio Max.

## 2002

### January 2002

In January 2002 I put a new design online, version 2 of the website. The first version had built its layout with a few table cells. This one relied on the spacer GIFs common at the time, which were used to draw the borders of boxes.

I had already built parts of the second version with PHP, so that I only had to maintain recurring elements once.

As was common on many sites back then, there was a guest book, a forum, and a counter. I had built the navigation with JavaScript, and it was very hard to use. At times I also used Flash for a <q>great</q> light effect and for a pure Flash home page with music and animations.

Along with a questionable choice of fonts and a <q>hip</q> style, I introduced the bamboo as the website's trademark for the first time.

## 2004

### September 2004

In September 2004 I created version 3. By now I understood more about design, and the website no longer looked horrible. Instead of a huge header image, I now used a stylized bamboo. Lacking the craft skills, I had <q>found</q> it somewhere on the internet and then worked it over with various photo filters and changes until it could pass as my <q>own creation</q>, to protect against copyright infringement.

Over the internet, I also had an artist carve a Japanese seal stone for me. I remember it took him a lot of effort to find the characters of kogakure in Chinese seal script.

Technically, this was the first time I used a semantic CSS layout, which arranged the page's columns with floats.

## 2006

### April 30, 2006

Two years later, content management systems were all the rage, and I bought a license for [ExpressionEngine](https://expressionengine.com/) to run my blog.

For version 4 of the website, I drew a completely new bamboo in Adobe Illustrator with the gradient mesh tool.

I also improved the fonts, and the design as a whole became lighter and fresher.

### August 25, 2006

#### Nomination for the Webdesignbook

The website was nominated for the **Webdesignbook**, a project that honored impressive designs. Nominated sites had to be built without tables and use valid markup and CSS. Once a year, a renowned jury selected 150 websites, which were published in the fall as a book of around 248 pages. The book also contained interviews with designers and articles about them, and the designs were ordered by color, style, and author. That year the jury consisted of Andy Budd, Tammie Lister, John Oxton, Marco van Hylckama Vlieg, James Archer, Kyle Neath, Chris J. Davis, and Thomas Silkjær.

### October 29, 2006

#### Bujinkan on <cite>Welt der Wunder</cite>

On Sunday, October 29, 2006, at 7:00 p.m., RTL2 broadcast a report about the ninja on its program <cite>Welt der Wunder</cite>.

For the report, the <cite>Welt der Wunder</cite> team visited Hatsumi Sensei in Japan and reenacted historical battles and ninja missions. Several German Bujinkan dōjō supported the film crew in front of and behind the camera.

I had the pleasure of providing the editors with the theoretical background before filming began.

## 2007

### February 25, 2007

#### The 100,000th Visitor

On February 25, 2007, the 100,000th visitor came to my website, counted since October 2005. Since counting began, 100,015 visitors had viewed a total of 497,349 pages.

The reports about ninja on RTL2 (<cite>Welt der Wunder</cite>) and PRO7 (<cite>Galileo Mystery</cite>) brought peak numbers. After the <cite>Welt der Wunder</cite> report, interest in the articles was many times greater than after the one on <cite>Galileo Mystery</cite>.

## 2008

### April 24, 2008

Version 5 of the website went online. Visually little changed; technically, everything did.

For the first time, I used a web framework. I had to write every single line myself and take care of the database, controllers, and much more besides the templates. Back then the Python web framework [Django](https://www.djangoproject.com/) won me over, so I taught myself Python and rebuilt the website from scratch. Django's admin interface was a particular highlight.

## 2010

### March 10, 2010

#### Cease-and-Desist Letter

My website had a small collection of sayings and quotes. Because of it, the heirs of **Karl Valentin** sent me a cease-and-desist letter for copyright infringement. I had to sign a declaration to cease and desist, but my lawyer was able to prevent greater damage. I paid the heirs nothing.

For the first time, I dealt with people whose whole purpose in life was making life hard for others. Karl Valentin's works entered the public domain at the start of 2019, and until then the heirs wanted to earn as much as possible.

I realized these were **commercial-scale cease-and-desist letters** when my lawyer told me that his Hamburg law firm alone represented four clients over this very saying.

After this experience, I had to take the sayings off the website entirely. I could not determine beyond doubt for each saying whether its author had been dead for more than 70 years.

## 2015

### April 7, 2015

Five years later, the sayings were back. I had checked every single author and removed all recent ones. Along with the sayings came a completely new design, version 6 of the website.

I had said goodbye to Django. Professionally I worked with Ruby, Python was only a hobby, and keeping a site like this running took a lot of effort.

Fortunately, many other developers had realized the same, and more and more of them were moving their websites and blogs to static pages. There is an almost unlimited number of [static site generators](https://www.staticgen.com/). They work with templates, logic, and data and still produce plain HTML pages in the end. No one can hack such a website. It needs no database or expensive, complicated server software, the pages can be hosted almost anywhere, and they scale superbly. Even a few thousand requests per second won't put a server under strain.

I used [Jekyll](https://jekyllrb.com/) as the site generator, [PostCSS](https://postcss.org/) for the CSS, [Browserify](http://browserify.org/) for the JavaScript, and [Gulp.js](https://gulpjs.com/) as the build tool.

The whole website was optimized for mobile and ran on any device.

I redrew the bamboo from scratch in [Affinity Designer](https://affinity.serif.com/designer/) so I could embed it directly in the page as SVG. SVG did not support the gradient mesh.

## 2017

### June 27, 2017

After six months of work, I published kogakure as an **eBook**.

I wrote the book with **GitBook**, which also handled the hosting and delivery of the eBook formats.

During the revision I fixed numerous errors, went through every article, added new images, and replaced graphics with SVG wherever possible.

### August 13, 2017

Jekyll rendered very slowly, and I had already moved my [private website](https://www.stefanimhoff.de) to the static site generator [Hugo](https://gohugo.io/). The next logical step was to do the same for kogakure.de.

Along the way, I reworked the whole website once more, made some smaller graphic changes, and completely redesigned the home page.

For the first time, I could use [CSS Grid Layout](https://gridbyexample.com/) on a larger scale. Working with this new CSS standard for page layouts was enormous fun.

The website now also had a service worker and could even be read without an internet connection.

## 2022

### February 9, 2022

In February 2022 I redesigned the website. It was based on my Japanese-inspired design following the aesthetic of _shibui_, which I had developed in 2020. I originally created it for my [private website](https://www.stefanimhoff.de) and later also used it for my [photo blog](https://hamburg.stefanimhoff.de). I reused the basics, such as the grid, the color palette, and the typography, but adjusted each site a little.

Technically, the site is now built with [Eleventy](https://www.11ty.dev/) and makes extensive use of CSS Grid Layout and CSS Custom Properties.
