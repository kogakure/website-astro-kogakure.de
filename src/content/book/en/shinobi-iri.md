If no one hears you, you don’t have to fight. **_Shinobi iri_** (忍び入り) means <q>moving forward silently</q> and is one way of avoiding a fight. The ninja knew various _ninja aruki_ (<q>step techniques</q>) for moving silently, unseen, or fast. Here are some of them.

## Overview of the Techniques

- _nuki ashi_ (抜き足) – silent step
- _ō ashi_ (大足) – big step
- _ko ashi_ (小足) – small step
- _ko kizami_ – short step
- _wari ashi_ – adapting step
- _tsune ashi_ (常足) – normal step
- _shime ashi_ (締め足) – firm step
- _yoko aruki_ (横歩き) – walking sideways
- _ayumi ashi_ (歩み足) – normal walking forward
- _nami ashi_ – taking a step
  - _mae ashi_ – forward
  - _ushiro ashi_ – backward
  - _yoko ashi_ – to the side
  - _naname ashi_ – diagonally to the side
- _hiraki ashi_ (開き足) – sliding sideways
- _okuri ashi_ (送り足) – following step
  - _suri ashi_ – draw up the rear foot, step out with the front
  - _yori ashi_ – gliding together
  - _tsugi ashi_ – step out with the front foot, draw up the rear
- _tobi ashi_ (跳び足) – flying step
  - _mae tobi_ – forward
  - _ushiro tobi_ – backward
  - _yoko tobi_ – to the side
  - _nidan geri_ – double kick
- _sashi ashi_ (差し足) – cross step
  - _mae ashi_ – forward
  - _ushiro ashi_ – backward
  - _yoko ashi_ – to the side
- _ashi fumikae_ – switching legs
- _tenkan ashi_ – turning step
- _chakuchi_ (着地) – lunge
  - _mae chakuchi_ – forward
  - _ushiro chakuchi_ – backward
  - _yoko chakuchi_ – to the side
- _chōkyori kyōsō_ (長距離競走) – long-distance running
- _heipo_ – the way of the salamander

## Techniques in Detail

### Nuki ashi – Silent Step

With _nuki ashi_ the ninja moves silently over wooden floorboards or straw mats, in full control of their body weight. From a low position, they slowly shift balance and weight onto the front leg until it carries the full load. Then they draw the rear leg forward, the ankles almost touching as it passes. The weight moves onto the new foot just as slowly. That way the ninja can pull the foot back at once on hearing a sound.

### Chōkyori kyōsō – Long-Distance Running

This running method was basic knowledge for every ninja, but each runner adapted it to their own build, stride length, and arm position.

The runner keeps the torso upright and the head straight and fixes on a point about 10 meters ahead. The foot lands on the outer edge of the heel and rolls forward. Breathing is the key to long distances: the ninja breathed from the diaphragm and kept a steady pace over the whole course.

According to some stories, ninja could cover the 560 kilometers between Edo (Tōkyō) and Ōsaka in three days.

### Ko ashi – Small Step

The small, stabbing step _ko ashi_ lets you walk through shallow water or leaves without a sound. The hips are low, the weight on the front foot. The ninja draws the rear foot forward and lifts it. Then they hold it above the spot where they want to step and test it carefully with the tip of the foot. Only when the spot is safe does the heel come down. The posture recalls a crane picking its way through the wild.

### Yoko aruki – Walking Sideways

With _yoko aruki_ the ninja moves quietly in the shadows of buildings or along corridors. The hips face sideways to the direction of travel. The weight goes onto the front foot, and the rear foot is placed in front of it. Then the weight shifts again and the rear foot is set down in its new position ahead. The movement is smooth and even, like a crab scuttling sideways along a beach.

### Heipo – The Way of the Salamander

<figure>
<img src="/images/iri/salamander.webp" alt="A masked ninja in dark clothing lies flat on the ground, supported only on hands and toes.">
<figcaption><em>Heipo</em></figcaption>
</figure>

With _heipo_ the ninja moves completely silently over a short distance. Lying flat on the ground, they move only on the palms and the tips of the toes. The technique also works on the fingers instead of the palms.

The arms stay as close to the body as possible to save strength. The further they spread out, the more strength it takes.

The body weight rests first on the right palm and the tip of the right toe and shifts rhythmically to the left side as the ninja moves, like a salamander.

Because the ninja touches the ground at only four points, noise is easy to avoid.

## Training

Training begins with observation: how do sounds arise in nature? Take plenty of time for this. Which sounds have which effect, what masks them, how do your own movements and those of others come across? Include sounds your subconscious usually filters out: doors, footsteps, wind.

Once you know how sounds arise, you simply make sure you don’t cause them. Then training moves to boards, leaves, grass, and gravel.

Stephen K. Hayes recommends laying a few layers of wet newspaper on the floor. If you step wrongly, you leave creases and folds in the surface.

These points help while learning:

- Lower your body weight and bend the knees slightly to keep control of your balance.
- Keep breathing as you move. Holding your breath tenses the muscles needlessly and costs you balance.
- Keep the whole movement in view. If you only watch your feet, you miss people and buildings.
- Use every joint. Don’t lock your knees or swing the whole leg stiffly from the hip.
- Keep your weight on the foot that is on the ground at all times. Never spread it over both feet.
- Hold your arms beside your body at different heights so that you notice an obstacle at once.
- If you make too much noise, stop and listen. Then lower your hips a little more, relax completely, breathe in deeply, and breathe out slowly as you move.
- Be patient. Unless speed is essential, take as much time as you need. Inattention leads to jerky movements, and those are easy to hear.
- Adapt your movements to the surroundings. In some places total silence stands out, and the occasional creak of the floorboards is only natural. Then move in time with that creaking.
