## The School of the Unmoved Heart

For a whole year, Takamatsu was simply thrown around. Only then did his grandfather Toda allow him to learn his first _kata_ (型) of **Shinden-Fudō-Ryū** (神伝不動流) _daken taijutsu_ (打拳体術).

### Origins

According to tradition, the Shinden-Fudō-Ryū was founded in the mid-12th century by Genpachiro Tameyoshi. Its roots lead back to the _koshi jutsu_ (骨指術) introduced by Izumo Kanja Yoshiteru. Whether this Izumo is the same person as the Izumo of the Kukishinden-Ryū (九鬼神伝流) is unclear.

### Training in Nature

The foundation of the teaching is knowledge of nature. Training begins by using nature to strengthen the body: first the legs and hips, then the fists.

This Ryū built no dōjō and made no training equipment. Training took place outdoors. Students practiced striking and breaking on trees, and then hardened their foreheads against them. In the bamboo forests they trained the kicks that only the Shinden-Fudō-Ryū knows. And they practiced tearing the bark off trees – a movement very similar to tearing off skin.

### Sword and Weapons

The school specializes in _jūjutsu_ (柔術) and _iainuki_ (居合抜き, <q>quick-drawing the sword</q>). The _katana_ (刀) of the Shinden-Fudō-Ryū is much longer and heavier than an ordinary one, which makes it very hard to draw. Once you had mastered it, you could draw a normal sword all the faster.

<img src="/images/article/shinden-fudo-ryu-02-drawing-long-sword.webp" alt="A swordsman in dark clothing, in a low stance, draws an exceptionally long sword from its scabbard, the blade halfway out.">
<figcaption>Drawing the long sword</figcaption>
</figure>

Izumo is said to have learned the _yari_ (槍) from the _tengu_ (天狗). These techniques remain secret to this day. The Ryū used various _yari_, as well as the _ono_ (斧, <q>war ax</q>), the _ōtsuchi_ (大槌, <q>war hammer</q>), and the _naginata_ (薙刀). _Hōjō jutsu_ (捕縄術, <q>the art of binding</q>) was part of it as well.

With the great war axes, fighters smashed samurai armor; with the war hammer, doors and barricades. The _naginata_ served mainly foot soldiers fighting against horsemen.

### Stance and Falling

The Shinden-Fudō-Ryū has no fixed _kamae_ (構え). Its hallmark is _shizen no kamae_ (自然の構え).

When thrown, you absolutely had to land on your feet, because in the Shinden-Fudō-Ryū you always wore the _daishō_ (大小). A fall with swords at your side was dangerous and could be fatal even with wooden swords.

A saying goes: anyone who wants to learn more than the basic techniques must be able to do a flip on a single finger.
