What the Dao (道) is can hardly be explained. The very first verse of Laozi’s <cite>Daodejing</cite> (道徳経) warns against trying:

> The Dao that can be spoken is not the eternal Dao. The name that can be named is not the eternal name.
>
> **Laozi**, <cite>Daodejing</cite>

Daoism is a way of life: moving with the course of time instead of against it. You can see this natural course in flowing water, in the flames of a fire, in clouds drifting across the sky.

Today people strive more than ever to dominate and control everything, and they do terrible things in the process. Daoism offers a good alternative.

Many people, however, find the teaching closed to them. The texts are almost all hard to understand and of great depth. Still, the old works of masters such as Laozi, Zhuangzi, Liezi, and other philosophers of their time are worth a look.

## Yin and Yang

Understanding Daoism starts with the polarity of yin and yang, in Japanese _in’yō_ (陰陽). It is a central part of the teaching. Industry and the media long ago turned its black-and-white symbol into a popular commodity, and many people wear it on a necklace without knowing what it means.

The West misreads the sign again and again. It does not stand for good and evil, for an eternal struggle between two forces, or for the Christian doctrine of heaven and hell. It shows a duality in which there is no _good_ and no _bad_. Each side is the flip side of the other: no _high_ without _low_, no _light_ without _dark_. Both are aspects of the same system. If one disappeared, so would the other.

The Western world, shaped by Christian teaching, tries instead to build a world of _joy without sorrow_, _wealth without poverty_, _health without disease_. That is why Western medicine mostly fights the symptoms of an illness rather than asking about its cause. A Chinese doctor looks for the cause first and then for a way to restore balance.

## With the Flow

Every invention meant to bring more of one thing automatically brings more of the other. Penicillin, nuclear power, cars, computers: whatever is invented to make the world _better_ often creates as many problems as it solves, or more. Daoism does not try to avoid either side. Both sides of the coin are right, because the universe accepts them. This is hard to grasp at first, especially for someone raised in a Christian world.

None of this means rejecting technology. Technology becomes destructive in the hands of people who do not see that they belong to the same process as everything else in the universe.

A hunter goes into the jungle. If he is wise, he shoots one monkey in a day, that is enough, and he goes home. A bad hunter shoots every monkey in the area and enjoys a lavish meal. When he wants to hunt a monkey again some time later, he has to walk twice as far to find any.

Whoever sees themselves as separate from the universe and tries to own or subdue it acts against the course of things. Sooner or later they fail, and so does their attempt to control everything.

Winds, tides, currents, and seasons show the principle of coming into being and passing away. A Daoist does not fight them. A Daoist uses them and goes <q>with the flow</q>.

## Dao

<q>There is no other way but the Way.</q> The Dao is the highest reality and power of the universe, the ground of being and non-being. It <q>does</q> nothing, yet leaves nothing <q>undone</q>. It cannot be defined in words; it is neither an idea nor a concept. It is not an energy pervading the universe, and it is not a god. It may have brought forth the gods, or God, but it is _not_ a god itself, and not a creator.

## So of Itself

The Dao is what happens of itself, in Chinese _ziran_ (自然): spontaneously, from within, without force.

You can neither _follow_ nor _not follow_ the Dao. You may imagine you stand outside the course of things, but that is only imagination.

The Dao, then, is the course of nature, the order of the universe. It generates itself, exists of itself, and is non-law.

Harmony arises in the universe when all things are allowed to go their own way. In ancient China a few rulers governed their states by the principles of the Dao, with as few laws, punishments, and officials as possible. In their time, it is said, peace in the land was at its strongest. Yet any attempt to put the Dao into words is doomed from the start.

## The Order of the Dao

Even so, the Dao has a structure and form, in Chinese _li_. It is an asymmetric order that never repeats itself and follows no rule. You can see it in flowing water, in the bark of a tree, in ice crystals, in pebbles on a beach, in muscle fibers, and in the shape of galaxies. _Li_ is a beauty that draws us in without our being able to explain it. Scientific explanations such as surface tension or mathematical laws do not capture this principle.

## Non-Action

The principle of Daoism is non-action, in Chinese _wu wei_ (無為). It does not mean inertia or laziness, laissez-faire or passivity. _Wu wei_ means not acting against the natural course of the Dao. It is the willow that yields under the weight of snow, not the tall, rigid tree whose branches break.

Whoever has understood the Dao lives _wu wei_. The ninja applied this principle too, as _in shin ton kei_: the greatest effect with the least effort. A ninja knew the laws of nature and of people so well that dealing with them cost only a minimum of energy.
