## Many Paths, One Truth

In the eternal course of the universe, a human being is little more than a brief spark. Yet each of us has a part of a greater truth to recognize and come to terms with. It need not be the same insight for everyone: each person asks a different question, and the same answer will not satisfy everyone.

Countless paths lead to _satori_ (悟り, <q>enlightenment</q>) and insight. Those who choose the path of knowledge may become scientists. The path of healing leads to work as a doctor or nurse. The path of rule leads into politics or to founding a company. There is also the path of the servant and the path of the merchant.

These are only a few of them. One is the path of the warrior, the path the _bujin_ (武人) have chosen.

## Finding Your Own Path

The first step is also the hardest: finding the right path and setting foot on it. In the past, birth decided the path. Today the choice is free, and that very freedom confuses some people, as does the sheer number of options. Some find their path in their youth and follow it without wavering until death. Others find it later, and some never do. Still others change paths several times until they find fulfillment.

Each person may arrive at a different answer. But one thing holds for every path: it is a means to an end and must never become an end in itself.

## The Path of the Warrior

Those who walk the path of the warrior may come to realize that all conflict is futile. They work on their strength and invulnerability, because only these give them the freedom to choose love and generosity.

At some point, many warriors on this path set out on a long wandering, the _musha shugyō_ (武者修行). It strips them of home, comfort, and an orderly daily routine, of friends, family, sometimes even their mother tongue. To cope, the ninja must be adaptable and resourceful.

Many things make it hard to enter this path and stay on it. The hardest is its exclusiveness: the warrior devotes themselves to a single aspect of existence and pursues it with full zeal every day of their life.

Someone who practices martial arts as a hobby is therefore not on the path of the warrior. That path is a lifelong commitment that consumes everything, never ends, and allows no deviation.

## What Enlightenment Is

The word comes easily and is hard to pin down. Enlightenment cannot be put into words. But when it begins to bloom in you, you will recognize it on your own.

The key is to let go of the ambitious pursuit of recognition and to lose yourself in an activity or situation that takes you in completely.

Enlightenment rarely comes all at once. More often it grows slowly. This sense of selflessness can be triggered by magnificent surroundings, by a meaningful event, or by shedding every constraint. In that moment of truth, you feel awe, joy, complete oneness, and vitality.

Afterward, you think differently. New insights grow from this that can fundamentally change how you live and act, and all oppressive heaviness can vanish from your life.

For the warrior, enlightenment is the realization that everything is illusion. Every thing and every situation, whether judged _good_ or _bad_, is _right_ and _fitting_ in the eyes of the universe.

## Name, Elements, Emptiness

One route to enlightenment is to free yourself from every bond that limits you. Symbolically, these bonds have three names: _name_, _elements_, and _emptiness_.

### Name

The **name** covers everything you have done up to this point and everything that was done to you, including everything you felt powerless against. It holds all the physical, social, family, and genetic baggage you have carried since birth. It also holds the talents and abilities you acquired over time, indeed the whole body and all its inclinations.

Shedding this bond means discarding every weakness you believed you had to live with. It means giving up all the excuses and self-limiting habits you kept out of convenience. Sentences like <q>I’m just fat</q>, <q>I don’t build muscle</q>, or <q>I’ll never learn this anyway</q> no longer exist from then on. This is far harder than it sounds.

### Elements

The **elements** are the way you respond to incidents. They include friends and enemies, the strengths and weaknesses you have acquired over time, and all cultural, political, and economic influences.

Letting go of them means throwing out all your habitual methods, prejudices, ideals, and blocking opinions, and rethinking your understanding of life. This is hard and takes a great deal of patience and perseverance.

### Emptiness

**Emptiness** stands for your own potential and power to shape and direct your life. Facing it means giving up the futile project of basing all actions, plans, and interactions on purely rational, mechanical principles.

If you cannot read the signs of the cosmos, you will fight a hopeless struggle all your life. Yet you can also fall into total helplessness by waiting your whole life for a sign from heaven, for helpful spirits to rescue you.

You can read as many books and self-help guides as you like: if your goal is not rooted deep within you, enlightenment remains out of reach.
