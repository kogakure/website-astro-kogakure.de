## School of the Jeweled Tiger

The Bujinkan (武神館) unites nine warrior traditions. The oldest of them is **Gyokko-Ryū** (玉虎流) _koshi jutsu_ (骨指術), and it is without question the core of most of the Bujinkan’s arts. Hatsumi Sensei established that _koshi jutsu_ is the foundation of these systems: Togakure-Ryū (戸隠流), Kotō-Ryū (虎倒流), Gikan-Ryū (義鑑流), Shinden-Fudō-Ryū (神伝不動流), Gyokushin-Ryū (玉心流), and Kumogakure-Ryū (雲隠流). Gyokko means <q>jeweled tiger</q> or <q>tiger jewel</q>, which may refer to the tiger’s eye.

### Origins in China

Its way of moving and its basic principles were developed in China during the Tang dynasty (618–907). The movements suggest that it was created by a small man or a small woman. According to tradition, a woman at the court in Chang’an (today’s Xi’an) was famous for her martial arts.

When the Tang dynasty was overthrown in 907, many nobles are said to have fled to Japan. The names Yo Gyokko (Yao Yu Hu) and Cho Gyokko are linked to the arrival of the martial art in Japan; perhaps they were one and the same person. General Ikai (or Ibou) is also associated with the origins of the Gyokko-Ryū. According to Hatsumi, the name could also simply mean <q>someone</q> (bou) <q>from a foreign country</q> (I).

### Tozawa Hakuunsai

The first true _sōke_ (宗家) of the official Gyokko-Ryū was Tozawa Hakuunsai. His name may point to a link with the Hakuun-Ryū, a school of ninjutsu that no longer exists. All this took place in the Hōgen era (1156–1159), which, according to the Bujinkan’s lineage records, makes the Gyokko-Ryū the oldest documented fighting system in Japan.

### Fighting Style

_Koshi jutsu_ means <q>the art of bone fingers</q>. Striking training is correspondingly hard: above all for the fingers, toes, and knuckles, but for every other part of the body as well. Typical of the style are powerful knuckle blocks that destroy the attacker’s muscles, tearing and drilling techniques with fingers and toes, heavy stomping kicks, grappling, and throws. At the same time, the Gyokko-Ryū taught its students to use only as much force as was needed to defeat the opponent. The school was also known for its skill with the sword, the _roku shaku bō_ (六尺棒), and the _tantō_ (短刀).

## The Nine Rules of Gyokko-Ryū

- The character _nin_ (忍) stands for protecting the nation, with your own life if need be.
- Put yourself aside, stay patient, have no fear of death.
- In danger, say nothing and show nothing.
- Faced with a strong enemy, keep your spirit unbending.
- Serve and protect your master as you would your parents.
- Crime scatters your abilities.
- Drunkenness clouds your judgment.
- Break the opponent’s strength, but let them live.
- Teach no one without the master’s permission.
