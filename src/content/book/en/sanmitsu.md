_Mikkyō_ (密教) recognizes three forces called the _sanmitsu_ (三密, <q>the three secrets</q>): thought, word, and deed. The _mandala_ (曼荼羅) stands for thought, the _mantra_ (真言) for word, the _mudrā_ (印相) for deed.

## Mandala

The Japanese counterpart of the _mandala_ is _nenriki_ (念力), focused intention. It embodies the will of the ninja.

_Ninpō mikkyō_ (忍法密教) looks at the structure of the universe in two ways that are completely opposed to each other. Only those who practice both gain insight.

### The View From Within

The first way observes the course of the universe from within. The ninja turns their attention to physical phenomena and seeks understanding from that standpoint.

<figcaption><em>Taizōkai mandara</em> (胎蔵界曼荼羅)</figcaption>
</figure>

This material world, the <q>world of the fertile ground</q>, is represented by the _taizōkai mandara_ (胎蔵界曼荼羅). The ninja contemplates it and reflects on the meaning of the artwork. In doing so, they realize that everything exists within this one universe: the galaxies, star clusters, star systems, stars, and planets, the animals and people, the words, colors, shapes, and thoughts.

Everything we do in our lives acts on this cycle. It is perceived because it takes place in God – and in turn, God is present in each of these things. The view widens to larger perspectives, and it may become clear that all opposites and paradoxes have their place in this universe.

### The View From Above

The second way lifts the mind above the universe. The ninja lets go of the self and surveys the whole. This is how they recognize the cosmic laws and grasp the fine threads that tie all things in the universe together and make them depend on one another.

<figure>
<img src="/images/historical/kongokai-mandara.webp" alt="A strictly geometric mandala on a dark blue ground: nine circles with seated Buddhas and deities are arranged in a grid of red and gold bands, with a Buddha with a golden halo at the center.">
<figcaption><em>Kongōkai mandara</em> (金剛界曼荼羅)</figcaption>
</figure>

This spiritual world, the <q>world of the diamond</q>, is represented by the _kongōkai mandara_ (金剛界曼荼羅). Here the diamond stands for hardness, clarity, and pure knowledge: the ultimate truth.

The ninja realizes that the reality they see is only a subjective view of what is real. Everything around us has only the form that our cosmic laws give it and that our consciousness shapes. Whoever leaves the material world behind and experiences the cosmic laws attains _kanjin kaname_ (肝心要), <q>the essential point</q>. The teaching of the Togakure-Ryū equates it with _shinshin shingan_ (神心神眼), <q>the mind and eyes of God</q>. From this higher, enlightened plane, one looks down on the earth.

### Both Ways

Practice only one of the two ways and you lose your balance. Either ninjutsu degenerates into a purely mechanical, technical fighting system without spirit, or you lose touch with reality, become unworldly, and feel above everything.

## Mantra

The Japanese counterpart of the _mantra_ is _jumon_ (呪文), the <q>charged</q> word. It embodies the intellect.

Words and deeds alone achieve nothing. They become effective only when the germinating thought behind them can be recognized as their motive. The charged word carries the thought on as a word. The words the ninja use are mostly still in their original language; where they have been translated, they carry no particular meaning.

Tantra holds that certain sounds can influence the course of the universe. Science has not yet explained exactly how this works. Yet many _mantra_ are very pure in their vibrations and are in harmony with the frequencies of physical reality.

### The Primal Sound

A simple example is the primal sound _Om_. The mouth opens wide for an <q>ah</q>. Without breaking the sound, it closes until an <q>oh</q> forms and then an <q>m</q>. Finally, the tongue presses against the front teeth, and an <q>n</q> sounds. The sequence is _ah-oh-mm-nn_, and it closely resembles the Christian prayer word <q>amen</q>.

Exercises like these can’t be learned alone. Pitch, breathing rhythm, and voice quality all shape the result. Anyone interested in _mantra_ should look for a qualified master.

## Mudrā

The Japanese counterpart of the _mudrā_ is _ketsuin_ (結印), the hand position. It embodies physical action.

The third secret of the _sanmitsu_ is the hand signs of the _kuji-in_ (九字印). They originate in the countless hand positions of India. Much like acupuncture, they probably stimulate certain meridians; the hands in particular are said to hold many end and turning points.

Only nine of these hand positions made their way into ninjutsu: _rin_ (臨), _pyō_ (兵), _tō_ (闘), _sha_ (者), _kai_ (皆), _jin_ (陣), _retsu_ (列), _zai_ (在), and _zen_ (前). These syllables are also the _mantra_ for each hand position. Each stands for a goal worth striving for, one that passes from thought through word into deed. The whole organism enters this state of harmony. The ninja feels as if the goal has already been reached and is only waiting to show itself on the physical plane.

Simply folding your hands into the shapes does nothing. Only many years of intensive training with an experienced master make the effect perceptible. Hollywood tells it differently: the wounded hero sits down in a corner, ties their hands in a knot, and is healed.

## Kuji-kiri

_Kuji-kiri_ (九字切り) is not part of the _sanmitsu_ and is included here for information only. It is closely linked to _kuji-in_, and most people confuse the two. Both probably came to Japan with Buddhism.

According to legend, _kuji-kiri_, also called _kuji goshin hō_ (九字護身法), comes from the godlike warrior guardian Marishiten. With this esoteric formula of power, the ninja overcomes evil, illusion, ignorance, or weakness and grows stronger. In doing so, the ninja imitates the flaming sword of _fudōmyō_, the fearsome mythical protector of the will, the laws, and the integrity of the universe.

<figure>
<img src="/images/kuji-kiri/kuji-kiri.webp" alt="A lattice of flat, interwoven bamboo strips with the character for demon painted over it in black ink.">
<figcaption><em>Kuji-kiri</em> (九字切り)</figcaption>
</figure>

The right, positive hand and the left, negative hand come together in front of the chest and form the sword. The ninja repeats an incantation several times. Then they step forward and cut through the air with the symbolic sword, alternating horizontal and vertical strokes, left to right and top to bottom, until the nine cuts form a grid. Meanwhile, the ninja speaks the nine syllables of _kuji goshin hō_. A tenth syllable may be added at will. Then a strike of the hand severs the grid, and the symbolic sword goes back into its _saya_ (鞘).

Fishermen liked to place the syllable for water in the grid as a tenth sign, painting it on paper for protection and a safe voyage. Ninja could add the sign for demon, for example, to strike fear into their enemies.

As with _kuji-in_, this description is for information only. Without the right teacher, long practice, the right breathing rhythm, and mental focus, _kuji-kiri_ has no effect at all.
