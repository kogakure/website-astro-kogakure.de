## The School of the Jeweled Heart

Little is known about the fighting techniques of the **Gyokushin-Ryū** (玉心流). The system focused less on combat than on espionage and other ninja skills. The school is known for the _nagenawa_ (投げ縄), the lasso.

<figcaption>The <em>nagenawa</em>, the throwing rope</figcaption>
</figure>

Gyokushin-Ryū _ninpō_ (忍法) is a branch of _koshi jutsu_ (骨指術). Its founder, Sasaki Goeman Teruyoshi, is believed to have been a student of the Gyokko-Ryū (玉虎流). The methods of the Gyokushin-Ryū – blocks, strikes, and stances – closely resemble those of the Gyokko-Ryū.

Sasaki Gendayu served the daimyō of Kishū. He received a stipend of 200 _koku_ (石) a year, later as much as 400. A _koku_ is a Japanese measure of rice equal to about 180 liters. Like his father, he was probably highly skilled in the Gyokko-Ryū.

The Gyokushin-Ryū was taught in secret, in Kishū Province and in the lands of the Takeda. Sometime in the 17th century it came into contact with the Togakure-Ryū (戸隠流) and the Toda family. Once the style had passed to Toda Nobutsuna, it was no longer kept secret.

Kanō Jigorō, the founder of jūdō (柔道), was a friend of Takamatsu Sensei. Takamatsu is believed to have taught the Gyokushin-Ryū as a guest at the Kōdōkan.
