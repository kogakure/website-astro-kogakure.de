The glossary explains **760** terms from ninjutsu, the martial arts, and Japanese history. All words are Japanese unless noted otherwise.

### aikidō

合気道 · <q>the way of harmony</q>

Defensive martial art developed by Ueshiba Morihei between 1925 and 1969.

### aikuchi

匕首

Small Japanese dagger.

### Ainu

アイヌ

Indigenous people of northern Japan, distinct from the Japanese in language and ancestry; their ancestors go back to the Jōmon culture. In Hokkaidō Prefecture’s 2023 survey, about 11,450 people identified as Ainu, and the number of their descendants is much higher. The Japanese long regarded them as barbarians and fought them for centuries, for example in Koshamain’s uprising of 1457 and Shakushain’s War of 1669–1672.

### aisha

愛者 · <q>loving person</q>

Principle of <em>gojō goyoku</em> that exploits a person’s compassion.

### Akechi Mitsuhide

明智光秀

General of Oda Nobunaga who betrayed his lord. When Nobunaga was without his bodyguards, Akechi attacked and killed him. His troops burned Nobunaga’s castle to the ground.

### akindo

商人 · <q>merchant</q>

A favorite ninja disguise from the <em>shichi hō de</em>.

### ametori no jutsu

雨鳥の術 · <q>the art of the birds in the rain</q>

Tactic that exploits the habit of guards to stay away from their posts, or keep watch half-heartedly, in rain or cold.

### amigasa

編笠

Woven straw hat whose wide brim hides the face.

### ashi

足 · <q>leg, foot</q>

### ashi fumikae

足踏み替え · <q>changing legs</q>

### ashi kō

足甲 · <q>armored feet</q>

Foot claws that ninja used to climb walls. The counterpart for the hands is called <em>shukō</em> or <em>tekagi</em>.

### ashigaru

足軽 · <q>light-footed soldier</q>

The lower class of the samurai. In peacetime most of them worked as farmers in the fields. Their weapons and armor were lighter and cheaper.

### Ashikaga

足利

Clan descended from the Minamoto that held the office of shōgun in Japan during the Ashikaga period (1338–1573).

### ashinaka

足半

Straw sandals without a heel that cover only the front of the foot. The toes grip the ground directly and find a sure footing even on slippery terrain.

### atemi

当身 · <q>strike to the body</q>

Punches and kicks against a person’s vital points and nerve centers to stun, paralyze, or kill.

### atemi waza

当身技 · <q>techniques of striking the body</q>

Another term for striking the vital points and nerve centers.

### atemite

当身手 · <q>striking the body with the hand</q>

Another term for striking the vital points and nerve centers.

### ayumi ashi

歩み足 · <q>normal walking</q>

### bajutsu

馬術 · <q>horsemanship</q>

### bakemono jutsu

化物術 · <q>spirit arts</q>

The art of turning oneself into a ghost. Igasaki Dōjun is said to have used it to get into a fortress.

### bakufu

幕府 · <q>tent government</q>

Originally the shōgun’s field camp, later the seat of his military dictatorship. In English, the <q>shogunate</q>.

### bakuhatsu gama

爆発鎌 · <q>bomb-throwing sickle</q>

Ninja weapon made of a large sickle (<em>kama</em>) and a two-meter chain with explosives, poison, or blinding powder at its end.

### Bansenshūkai

萬川集海 · <q>ten thousand rivers flow into the sea</q>

Ten-part compendium of ninjutsu, written in 1676 by Fujibayashi Yasutake.

### banzai

万歳 · <q>ten thousand years</q>

Japanese cry of joy and battle cry.

### bikenjutsu

秘剣術

Sword fighting. With <em>taijutsu</em> and <em>bōjutsu</em>, one of the three pillars of <em>ninpō</em>.

### bisentō

眉尖刀 · <q>halberd</q>

Japanese halberd with a broad blade.

### bisentōjutsu

Fighting with the halberd (<em>bisentō</em>). One of the 18 fields of the <em>ninja jūhakkei</em>.

### Biwako

琵琶湖 · <q>Lake Biwa</q>

The largest lake in Japan (about 670 km²), near Kyōto. It takes its name from its shape, which resembles a lute (<em>biwa</em>).

### bō

棒 · <q>stick, staff</q>

Term for all Japanese staff weapons; in the narrower sense, the <em>roku shaku bō</em>.

### bō ken

棒剣 · <q>stick sword</q>

Practice sword made of heavy wood, also <em>bokken</em> or <em>bokutō</em>. A dangerous weapon in its own right.

### bō shuriken

棒手裏剣

Long, dagger-like throwing weapons (<em>shuriken</em>), among them hairpins, nails, and chopsticks.

### bōbi no kamae

防備の構え

Defensive stance (<em>kamae</em>).

### bōenkyō

望遠鏡 · <q>telescope</q>

### bōjutsu

棒術

Staff fighting. With <em>taijutsu</em> and <em>bikenjutsu</em>, one of the three pillars of <em>ninpō</em>.

### bokken

木剣 · <q>wooden sword</q>

Also <em>bō ken</em> or <em>bokutō</em>.

### boku

木 · <q>tree, wood</q>

Also <em>moku</em>.

### bokutō

木刀 · <q>wooden sword</q>

Also <em>bō ken</em> or <em>bokken</em>.

### bōryaku

謀略 · <q>strategy</q>

Part of Japanese warfare (<em>heihō</em>) that includes, among other things, the battle formations.

### boshi ken

母指拳 · <q>thumb fist</q>

Strike that hits the target with the tip of the thumb.

### bu

武 · <q>art of war, warriorship, military</q>

Also <em>mu</em>.

### Buddha

बुद्ध · <q>the awakened one</q>

<em>Sanskrit</em>. Term for a person who has broken the cycle of rebirth. Usually it means Siddhārtha Gautama, a prince from Kapilavastu and the founder of Buddhism. In Japanese, <em>butsu</em>.

### budō

武道 · <q>martial arts</q>

Umbrella term for all Japanese martial arts.

### budōka

武道家 · <q>martial artist</q>

Someone who practices <em>budō</em>.

### bugei jūhappan

武芸十八般

The 18 categories of martial arts. A ninja needed a good grounding in them; on top of that came the 18 categories of secret techniques of the <em>ninja jūhakkei</em>.

### bugeisha

武芸者 · <q>master of the martial arts</q>

Term for the warriors (samurai) who practiced the martial arts. Also <em>bujin</em>.

### bujin

武人 · <q>military person</q>

Also <em>bugeisha</em>.

### Bujinkan

武神館 · <q>the hall of the divine warrior</q>

Organization founded by Hatsumi Masaaki that unites nine old warrior and ninjutsu Ryū.

### bujutsu

武術 · <q>the martial arts, wushu</q>

Term for all martial arts of the samurai.

### buke

武家 · <q>samurai, warrior, warrior family</q>

Term for the noble warrior families.

### bukkyō

仏教 · <q>Buddhism</q>

### bushi

武士 · <q>warrior, samurai</q>

Term for the noble warriors of feudal Japan.

### bushidō

武士道 · <q>the way of the warrior</q>

Code of honor of the samurai that demanded self-discipline, self-sacrifice, and self-control.

### butsu

仏 · <q>Buddha</q>

### chakuchi

着地 · <q>lunge</q>

### chi

地 · <q>earth, ground</q>

### chi no kata

地の型 · <q>earth form</q>

### Chi ryaku no maki

地略の巻 · <q>scroll of the strategies of the earth</q>

Book on the strategies of the earth, part of a series with <cite>Ten ryaku no maki</cite> and <cite>Jin ryaku no maki</cite>.

### chigiriki

乳切木

Long staff with a chain and an iron weight at the end, a weapon that evolved from the flail.

### chikairi

近入 · <q>close entry</q>

Strategies that ninja used in war to break through enemy lines.

### chiken-in

智拳印 · <q>sign of the fist of wisdom</q>

Hand sign for <em>retsu</em> in <em>kuji-in</em>.

### chikujō jutsu

築城術 · <q>the art of building a castle</q>

Methods of field fortification.

### chikyū jutsu

弛弓術 · <q>slack bow</q>

Tactic of <em>tōiri</em>. A captured ninja accepts the enemy lord’s offer of work and escapes at the first opportunity.

### chimon

地文 · <q>geography</q>

### chitsu mishi

<q>paid eyes</q>

Inhabitants of enemy territory who work for a ninja.

### chōda

長蛇 · <q>long snake</q>

Battle formation from the <em>bōryaku</em>.

### chōhō

諜報 · <q>espionage</q>

### chōkyori kyōsō

長距離競走 · <q>long-distance run</q>

Running technique that ninja used to carry messages over long distances.

### chūnin

中忍 · <q>middle ninja</q>

Middle rank of the ninja, the link between a clan leader (<em>jōnin</em>) and the agents in the field (<em>genin</em>). Because the <em>chūnin</em> usually made contact with the client, their strengths lay in judging people and in disguise.

### dai

大 · <q>big</q>

### daikongō-in

大金剛輪印 · <q>sign of the great diamond</q>

Hand sign for <em>pyō</em> in <em>kuji-in</em>.

### daimyō

大名 · <q>great name</q>

The rising great landowners who fought for power in Japan during the Sengoku jidai.

### daisharin

大車輪 · <q>great cartwheel</q>

Ninja weapon made of an axle and two large wheels, rolled into the opponent’s legs.

### daishō

大小 · <q>big and small</q>

The pair of swords of the samurai, <em>katana</em> and <em>wakizashi</em>.

### daitō

大刀 · <q>great sword</q>

Term for the samurai’s long sword, as opposed to the short sword.

### daken taijutsu

打拳体術 · <q>the art of striking the body with the fist</q>

Fighting techniques from <em>taijutsu</em>: kicks, punches, and blocks. They divide into <em>koppō jutsu</em> and <em>koshi jutsu</em>.

### dan

段 · <q>rank, level</q>

Term for the master grades.

### Dao

道 · <q>the Way</q>

<em>Chinese</em>. In Daoism, the highest reality and power of the universe, the ground of being and non-being. It cannot be defined in words.

### Daodejing

道徳経

<em>Chinese</em>. Founding text of Daoism, traditionally attributed to Laozi.

### Dengyō Daishi

伝教大師

Founder of the Japanese Tendai sect of Buddhism and of the Enryakuji monastery, also known as Saichō.

### densho

伝書 · <q>transmitted writings</q>

Secret documents of a Ryū that record its techniques and rules.

### do

土 · <q>earth, ground</q>

### dō

道 · <q>way, path, principle</q>

Term for the Asian worldview.

### dō no hi

銅の火 · <q>copper fire</q>

Another name for the pocket warmer <em>dōka</em>.

### dōjō

道場 · <q>training hall</q>

### dōka

胴火 · <q>body fire</q>

Pocket warmer, a small stove into which the ninja put a piece of smoldering charcoal. Also <em>dō no hi</em>.

### dokko-in

独鈷印 · <q>sign of the Tibetan vajra thunderbolt</q>

Hand sign for <em>rin</em> in <em>kuji-in</em>.

### doko no kamae

怒虎の構え · <q>angry tiger</q>

Stance (<em>kamae</em>) from ninjutsu.

### dōkyō

道教 · <q>Daoism</q>

Teaching and religion from China; in Japanese the philosophical school is more precisely called <em>dōka</em> (道家). It teaches a turn toward nature and an understanding of the universe.

### dosha

怒者 · <q>angry person</q>

Principle of <em>gojō goyoku</em> that exploits a person’s irritability.

### doteki

動的 · <q>dynamic</q>

Dynamics, the most effective application of mechanics (<em>kikaigaku</em>). It includes the proper use of energy, rhythm and flow, strength and flexibility, and the ability to feel an action and adapt to it.

### doton

土遁

Escape by means of earth, one of the five methods of <em>gotonpō</em>.

### doton jutsu

土遁術 · <q>the art of escaping with earth</q>

Escape technique from <em>gotonpō</em> that uses earth as a means of escape.

### ebira

箙 · <q>quiver</q>

### Edo

江戸

Former name of the Japanese capital, today Tōkyō.

### eiku

櫂

Boat oar used as a weapon in Okinawan <em>kobudō</em>. A ninja disguised as a fisherman could put up a good defense with it.

### eki

易 · <q>oracle</q>

Consulted with coins or yarrow stalks, as in the Chinese <cite>I Ching</cite>.

### endoku ken

煙毒剣 · <q>smoke-poison blade</q>

Throwing star (<em>shuriken</em>) that could be set alight and then gave off poisonous gases.

### engetsu

偃月 · <q>crescent moon</q>

Battle formation from the <em>bōryaku</em>.

### ennyū no jutsu

<q>mock attack</q>

A squad stages a feint assault. While the defenders rush to meet it, the ninja slips into the fortress unhindered.

### Enryakuji

延暦寺

Head monastery of the Tendai sect on Mount Hiei near Kyōto, founded at the behest of Tennō Kammu as the center of Tendai Buddhism. At its height, up to 40,000 monks and <em>sōhei</em> are said to have lived in 3,000 temples on the mountain. In September 1571, Oda Nobunaga destroyed the monastery during his campaign against the Ikkō ikki, the ninja, and the <em>sōhei</em>, and killed most of its inhabitants.

### fū

風 · <q>wind</q>

### fū no kata

風の型 · <q>wind form</q>

### fudō ken

不動拳 · <q>immovable fist</q>

A way of making a fist found in many martial arts: the <q>normal</q> fist.

### fudōmyō

不動明王

Fudō Myōō, the fearsome mythical protector of the will, the laws, and the integrity of the universe. In <em>kuji-kiri</em>, the ninja imitates his flaming sword.

### fudōshin

不動心 · <q>immovable heart</q>

A calm, unshakable mind.

### fudōza no kamae

不動座の構え · <q>immovable seat</q>

Seated posture in Japan and also a stance (<em>kamae</em>) in ninjutsu.

### Fujibayashi Nagato

藤林長門

Famous <em>jōnin</em> from the Iga region. According to a popular theory, just a false identity of Momochi Sandayū.

### Fujita Seiko

藤田西湖

Grandmaster of ninjutsu who called himself the 14th and last head of the Kōga-Ryū; he named no successor. He died in Tōkyō on January 4, 1966.

### fukibari

吹き針 · <q>blowing needles</q>

Ninja fighting technique of blowing small metal needles (<em>fukumi bari</em>) into the attacker’s face.

### fukiya

吹き矢 · <q>blowgun and dart</q>

The darts were usually poisoned.

### fukumi bari

含み針 · <q>small darts</q>

Small metal needles blown in <em>fukibari</em>.

### fukuro gaeshi jutsu

袋返術 · <q>the turned bag</q>

Tactic of <em>tōiri</em>. After what looks like a quarrel, the ninja changes sides and serves a new master for a while. When the war reaches a decisive phase, the ninja carries out the mission and returns to the old master.

### fukuro gaeshi zen jutsu

袋翻全術

Tactic of <em>chikairi</em>. The ninja intercepts messages between the enemy lord and his allies, forges them, and sends them on to sow distrust between them.

### Fūma Kotarō

風魔小太郎

Famous ninja of <em>jōnin</em> rank with, according to tradition, 200 followers, a specialist in guerrilla warfare.

### furyō

不良 · <q>young delinquents</q>

Good-for-nothings, young thugs.

### futokoro teppō

懐鉄砲 · <q>pocket gun</q>

Bronze pistol that was easy to conceal and popular with the ninja.

### gandō

龕灯 · <q>Buddhist altar light</q>

Lamp whose light source always stays upright.

### gankō

雁行 · <q>flight of wild geese</q>

Battle formation from the <em>bōryaku</em>.

### gasshō no kamae

合掌の構え · <q>prayer stance</q>

Stance (<em>kamae</em>) from ninjutsu, a greeting with the palms pressed together.

### gebaku ken-in

外縛拳印 · <q>sign of the outer-bound fist</q>

Hand sign for <em>kai</em> in <em>kuji-in</em>, the same sign used in prayer.

### gedan no kamae

下段の構え

Low stance (<em>kamae</em>).

### geinyū jutsu

迎入術

Tactic of <em>chikairi</em>. The ninja enters enemy territory to spread confusion, fear, and terror, for example with false rumors, arson, and sabotage.

### gekkenjutsu

撃剣術

Sword fighting. Listed together with <em>kenjutsu</em> in the <em>bugei jūhappan</em>.

### genin

下忍 · <q>lower ninja</q>

Lowest rank of the ninja. These agents in the field took their orders from the <em>chūnin</em> and usually did not know the <em>jōnin</em>. Their strengths were close combat, concealment, infiltration, and sabotage.

### Genji

源氏

Another name for the Minamoto.

### genjutsu

幻術 · <q>art of illusion</q>

### genmai

玄米

Unpolished rice.

### Genpei kassen

源平合戦

The Genpei War (1180–1185) between the Taira and the Minamoto.

### geri

蹴り · <q>kick</q>

Also <em>keri</em>.

### geta

下駄

Wooden sandals raised on tall supports, with a cross strap or a toe strap.

### gi

着 · <q>clothing, outfit, uniform</q>

Training clothes.

### Gikan-Ryū

義鑑流 · <q>school of learning justice</q>

One of the nine Ryū of the Bujinkan.

### gisō jutsu

擬装術 · <q>the art of imitation and disguise</q>

The art of putting oneself in another person’s place and taking on a new identity. Part of <em>onshin jutsu</em>.

### godai

五大 · <q>the great five</q>

The five elements of the universe in <em>mikkyō</em>: earth (<em>chi</em>), water (<em>sui</em>), fire (<em>ka</em>), wind (<em>fū</em>), and emptiness (<em>kū</em>).

### godan

五段 · <q>fifth dan</q>

In the Bujinkan test for <em>godan</em>, the teacher approaches the student silently with a sword. The student has to sense the danger and evade the stroke.

### gogyō

五行 · <q>theory of the five elements</q>

View of nature taken over from Chinese Daoism: water (<em>sui</em>), wood (<em>moku</em>), fire (<em>ka</em>), earth (<em>do</em>), and metal (<em>kin</em>).

### gogyō no kata

五行の型 · <q>form of the five movements</q>

### gogyō setsu

五行説 · <q>theory of the five elements</q>

Short form <em>gogyō</em>.

### gojō goyoku

五情五欲

Ninja strategy of steering people through their character traits. Its five principles are <em>aisha</em>, <em>dosha</em>, <em>kisha</em>, <em>kyōsha</em>, and <em>rakusha</em>.

### goku no kamae

Stance (<em>kamae</em>) from ninjutsu.

### Gorin no sho

五輪の書 · <q>the book of five rings</q>

Miyamoto Musashi’s work on swordsmanship and strategy.

### gorintō

五輪塔

Stone monument of five stacked parts – cube, sphere, pyramid, half-moon, and jewel.

### goshin no kata

梧心の型 · <q>form of the phoenix-tree heart</q>

### goton jutsu

五遁術 · <q>art of the five escapes</q>

Another name for <em>gotonpō</em>.

### gotonpō

五遁法 · <q>the five methods of escape</q>

The ninja principle of escaping with the help of the five elements of <em>gogyō</em>.

### gu

具 · <q>tool</q>

### gunryaku heihō

Military strategy. One of the 18 fields of the <em>ninja jūhakkei</em>.

### gyaku nagare

逆流れ · <q>reverse flow</q>

Breakfall (<em>ukemi</em>) from <em>ichimonji no kamae</em>.

### gyaku nage

逆投げ · <q>reverse throw</q>

Throw with a joint lock.

### gyōja no kamae

行者の構え · <q>ascetic’s stance</q>

Stance (<em>kamae</em>) in which a long staff is held upright beside the body like a walking stick.

### gyōkaku ken

<q>united horns</q>

The index, middle, and ring fingers form a trident whose tips stab into soft parts of the body such as the neck.

### Gyokko-Ryū

玉虎流 · <q>school of the jeweled tiger</q>

One of the nine Ryū of the Bujinkan.

### gyokuro

玉露

High-grade green tea. Ninja are said to have made a poison from it by burying the tea in the ground for 30 to 40 days and then mixing it with soybean paste. A sick person died of it within a few days, a healthy one after 70 days at most.

### Gyokushin-Ryū

玉心流 · <q>the school of the jeweled heart</q>

One of the nine Ryū of the Bujinkan.

### gyorin

魚鱗 · <q>fish scale</q>

Battle formation from the <em>bōryaku</em>.

### ha jutsu

破術 · <q>the art of breaking</q>

Escaping from holds.

### Hagakure

葉隠 · <q>hidden leaves</q>

Book by the samurai Yamamoto Tsunetomo on the philosophical side of <em>bushidō</em>.

### hakama

袴

Traditional divided skirt.

### han

藩 · <q>feudal domain, fief, province</q>

The fief of a daimyō.

### hanbō

半棒 · <q>half staff</q>

Its proper name is <em>san shaku bō</em>.

### hanbōjutsu

半棒術

Fighting with the short staff (<em>hanbō</em>).

### hankyū

半弓 · <q>half bow</q>

Short bow of the ninja, sometimes collapsible and easy to carry.

### hanmi no kamae

半身の構え · <q>half body</q>

Stance (<em>kamae</em>) from ninjutsu.

### happa ken

八葉拳 · <q>eight-leaf fist</q>

### happō hiken

八法秘剣 · <q>eight methods and secret swords</q>

The eight methods (<em>happō</em>) and the secret sword techniques (<em>hiken</em>) of the Kukishinden-Ryū.

### happō hikenjutsu

八法秘剣術

See <em>happō hiken</em>.

### hara

腹 · <q>belly</q>

The whole area between the stomach and the lower abdomen, regarded as the center of mental and physical strength.

### hasami bune

Collapsible ninja boat used to carry equipment across water. The <cite>Bansenshūkai</cite> describes such a folding boat as the <em>hasamibako bune</em> (挟箱船): its boards were joined by hinges, and folded up it fit into a carrying chest. Also <em>tatami bune</em>.

### hashigo

梯子 · <q>ladder</q>

Usually made of reed or bamboo. In compounds <em>bashigo</em>, as in <em>kumo bashigo</em>.

### hashigo yari

梯子槍 · <q>ladder spear</q>

Long pole with pegs projecting in pairs and an iron hook at the top, used as a ladder.

### Hatsumi Masaaki

初見良昭

Born Hatsumi Yoshiaki. Founder of the Bujinkan Budō Dōjō, <em>sōke</em> of the nine Ryū of the Bujinkan, and student of Takamatsu Toshitsugu.

### Hattori Hanzō

服部半蔵

Famous <em>jōnin</em> from the Iga region who was also a samurai in the service of Tokugawa Ieyasu.

### Hayes, Stephen K.

Teacher of ninjutsu and student of Hatsumi Masaaki. He was the first non-Japanese to become a <em>shidōshi</em>. In the United States he founded the Tōshindō school.

### heihō

兵法 · <q>method of the warrior</q>

Covers combat methods as well as combat strategies (<em>bōryaku</em>).

### Heike

平家

Another name for the Taira.

### heipo

平歩 · <q>way of the salamander</q>

Ninja crawling method.

### hensō jutsu
