Anyone who wants to train in the Bujinkan has to accept its guidelines. They were issued by Dr. Hatsumi Masaaki. They set out who is admitted, who has to leave, and who bears the responsibility when an accident happens.

## Who May Train

The Bujinkan Budō is open to everyone who agrees with the guidelines and supports them. Those who do not agree are refused entry.

Only those who practice true patience, self-control, and dedication are admitted. People with mental illness, drug addiction, or mental instability may not take part. The guidelines require a medical examination of anyone who could be a danger to others: people with infectious diseases, with a clinically abnormal personality or physiology, and people who lack self-control.

## Who Is Turned Away

People with a criminal record are turned away, as are troublemakers and anyone who has committed a crime.

Anyone who breaks the guidelines and commits shameful or criminal acts, whether as a practitioner or as a member of society, is expelled as well. Hatsumi explains this rule with past experience. Until then, the Bujinkan had been open to a great many people who came to Japan. Unfortunately, among them were people who turned violent when drunk, mentally ill people, and troublemakers who thought only of themselves and did not notice how others rejected their behavior. They soiled the righteous heart of the Bujinkan. From this day on, the guidelines state, everyone of this kind will be expelled.

## Accidents and Responsibility

The guidelines expressly call this point very important: if an accident happens during training, in the dōjō or outside it, it must not cause the Bujinkan any problems. Anyone who does not accept personal responsibility for accidents in Bujinkan training is not admitted. For clarity, the guidelines repeat it once more: the Bujinkan takes no responsibility for accidents of any kind during training, wherever they happen.

## The Membership Card

Anyone who wants to join the Bujinkan must buy an annual membership card. It protects the honor of Bujinkan members. And it shows that its holder is part of a greater whole, whose members come together with a warrior’s heart to improve themselves through training and friendship.

## Taijutsu and Peace

The Bujinkan tradition recognizes what is natural and shared in all human life. It is aware of what flows naturally between two parts. The first concerns _taijutsu_ (体術): <q>The secret principle of <em>taijutsu</em> is knowing the basis of peace.</q> The second concerns learning. It is the path of the unshakable heart, _fudōshin_ (不動心).

## An International Bujinkan

In recent times the Bujinkan has become international. Just as there are different time zones, there are different prohibitions among peoples and nations. Members are to respect one another and try to avoid such prohibitions. The warrior’s heart comes first, working together to improve both the individual and the Bujinkan.
