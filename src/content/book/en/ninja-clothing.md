The ninja suit was called _shinobi shōzoku_ (忍び装束) or _ninniku yoroi_. It was made of dark, coarse cloth in gray, dark blue, or black, white-gray in winter, and covered almost the whole body. In the dark it let the ninja melt into the shadows.

<figure>
<img src="/images/tools/shinobi-shozoku.webp" alt="A dark indigo cotton jacket, trousers, gaiters, hood, and face mask, laid out on the left and worn on the right, fastened with cord ties.">
<figcaption><em>Shinobi shōzoku</em> (忍び装束)</figcaption>
</figure>

Yet the suit was rarely worn. Many missions went better in ordinary clothes that drew less attention. Ninja caught in the suit faced certain death. Those caught dressed as fishermen or farmers had a much easier time explaining themselves.

According to more recent findings, the suit wasn’t pitch black either. At night, a black surface stands out more than a dark gray one.

The mask known today was most likely rarely used. It muffles hearing, and on a dark night hearing mattered. Usually the ninja just tied a simple cloth over the mouth.

In some battles, ninja fought alongside the troops in full battle gear. Then they wore chain mail, the _kusari katabira_ (鎖帷子), under or over the _shinobi shōzoku_. The mail covered the face and the palms as well.

<img src="/images/tools/kusari-katabira.webp" alt="A shirt with separate sleeves made of small riveted chain rings sewn onto indigo cloth.">
<figcaption><em>Kusari katabira</em> (鎖帷子)</figcaption>
</figure>

## Amigasa

<figure>
<img src="/images/tools/amigasa.webp" alt="A broad, shallow conical straw hat with a deep brim that shadows the face, and a chin cord.">
<figcaption><em>Amigasa</em> (編笠)</figcaption>
</figure>

The woven straw hat _amigasa_ (編笠) hid the face under its wide brim. The <cite>Shōninki</cite> of 1681 counts it among the six basic tools for a mission. Disguised as a monk or traveler, a ninja could walk through town under it unrecognized.

## Ashinaka

The _ashinaka_ (足半) were straw sandals without a heel that covered only the front of the foot. The toes gripped the ground directly, which gave a surer footing on slippery terrain. Warriors wore them on the battlefield as early as the 13th century, and Oda Nobunaga is said to have always carried a pair on his belt. The ninja manuals do not mention them.

## Geta

<figure>
<img src="/images/tools/geta.webp" alt="Wooden platform sandals raised on two tall crosswise teeth, with a cloth toe thong.">
<figcaption><em>Geta</em> (下駄)</figcaption>
</figure>

Under their _tabi_ (足袋), the ninja usually wore wooden sandals, the _geta_ (下駄), also called _yuki waraki_. In winter they fastened blades of steel or wood beneath them to walk safely on fresh snow and ice. In doing so, the ninja trained one of the most important skills: balance.

## Gi

<figure>
<img src="/images/tools/gi.webp" alt="A plain indigo cotton jacket and trousers with a cloth belt, laid out flat.">
<figcaption><em>Gi</em> (着)</figcaption>
</figure>

The _gi_ (着) is the training uniform in which martial arts are practiced today: a cotton jacket and trousers, held together by a belt. It has little in common with the historical ninja suit.

## Hakama

<figure>
<img src="/images/tools/hakama.webp" alt="Deeply pleated, wide-legged trousers in dark indigo with a stiff back plate and long waist ties.">
<figcaption><em>Hakama</em> (袴)</figcaption>
</figure>

The ninja’s trousers, the _hakama_ (袴), were tied at the knees and ankles. This gave more freedom of movement.

## Hokamuri

_Hokamuri_ (頬被り) is another name for the _tenugui_.

## Jikatabi

<figure>
<img src="/images/tools/tabi.webp" alt="Indigo cotton boots with a separate big toe, thick stitched soles, and small metal clasps up the side.">
<figcaption><em>Tabi</em> (足袋)</figcaption>
</figure>

The _jikatabi_ (地下足袋) are shoes with a separate big toe and a non-slip sole. The modern rubber-soled form dates only from the 20th century, but even their cloth forerunners gave a secure footing, allowed quiet movement, and made climbing easier. With them, ninja could get up a rope quickly: they wedged the rope between the big toe and the other toes and <em>walked</em> up.

## Kabuto

<figure>
<img src="/images/tools/kabuto.webp" alt="A dark lacquered iron helmet of a few riveted plates with a modest neck guard of lacquered plates laced with dark silk, small side flaps, and a hemp chin cord.">
<figcaption><em>Kabuto</em> (兜)</figcaption>
</figure>

The _kabuto_ (兜) was the helmet of Japanese armor. Ordinary warriors wore plain versions made of a few riveted iron plates with a neck guard, without the large crests of the commanders. There is no evidence that ninja wore such helmets. When they went into battle as warriors, they probably wore the same gear as everyone else.

## Kigomi

<figure>
<img src="/images/tools/shinobi-yoroi.webp" alt="Quilted cloth armor with small panels of chain mail and lacquered plates sewn between the layers.">
<figcaption><em>Kigomi</em> (着込み)</figcaption>
</figure>

Armor worn under ordinary clothes was called _kigomi_ (着込み). A common form was the _kusari katabira_ (鎖帷子), a cloth shirt with chain mail sewn in; some versions also had small lacquered plates between the quilted layers. Warriors and travelers wore such armor. There is no evidence that ninja wore it regularly.

## Obi

<figure>
<img src="/images/tools/obi.webp" alt="A long folded sash of coarse indigo cotton, wound and knotted.">
<figcaption><em>Obi</em> (帯)</figcaption>
</figure>

The ninja did not actually use a belt, the _obi_ (帯). It was added only in more recent times and today shows whether someone is a student or a master. Instead, a kind of belt was already sewn onto the trousers.

## Tebukuro

<figure>
<img src="/images/tools/tebukuro.webp" alt="Indigo cotton gloves with separate forearm covers, tied with cords at the wrist.">
<figcaption><em>Tebukuro</em> (手袋)</figcaption>
</figure>

The handguard _tebukuro_ (手袋) covered the backs of the hands and the forearms. With clenched fists, nothing of the arms could be seen in the dark.

## Tenugui

<figure>
<img src="/images/tools/kaiki.webp" alt="A large, plain, loosely folded cloth of coarse, hand-woven black cotton.">
<figcaption><em>Tenugui</em> (手拭)</figcaption>
</figure>

The _tenugui_ (手拭), also called _hokamuri_, was a cloth about 80 centimeters long. It could be tied into a mask or rolled out on the floor so the boards didn’t creak underfoot. And in the dark, when the ninja couldn’t see what the water looked like, they filtered drinking water through it. For this purpose, the fibers are said to have contained antibacterial substances.

## Uwagi

<figure>
<img src="/images/tools/uwagi.webp" alt="An indigo cotton wrap jacket with narrow sleeves and cord ties at the side.">
<figcaption><em>Uwagi</em> (上着)</figcaption>
</figure>

The jacket _uwagi_ (上着) was tucked into the trousers. It had hidden inner pockets in which _shuriken_ (手裏剣), for example, could be concealed.

## Yuki Waraki

_Yuki waraki_ is another name for the _geta_.
