Some ninja simply sawed their sword blades shorter so they could move better with them. To the samurai, who held their swords in special regard, this was an outrage. To a ninja, a weapon was no more than a tool for reaching a goal.

The ninja added a large pool of weapons to the unarmed _taijutsu_ (体術). They adapted quickly to new circumstances and improved or modified their weapons until they were more useful. They saw the value of firearms early, long before the first daimyō could bring themselves to use them.

What follows is a small selection of weapons the ninja liked to use, and by no means a complete one.

## Aikuchi

The _aikuchi_ (匕首) is a small dagger that closely resembles the _tantō_ (短刀). It was worn in the _obi_ (帯).

## Ashi Kō

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/ashi-ko.webp" alt="A forged iron band worn on the foot, with four tapered spikes projecting from its plate beneath the sole.">
<figcaption><em>Ashi kō</em></figcaption>
</figure>

The counterpart to the _shukō_ (手鉤) is the _ashi kō_: a device like the one worn on the hand, fastened under the ninja’s foot instead. It made a kick far more damaging, and it made climbing much easier.

## Bakuhatsu Gama

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/bakuhatsu-gama.webp" alt="A sickle with a short wooden handle, a long iron chain running from the butt to a round explosive charge with a fuse.">
<figcaption><em>Bakuhatsu gama</em></figcaption>
</figure>

To the end of the _bakuhatsu gama_ the ninja attached explosives, poison, or blinding powder. The ninja hurled it into the attacker’s face, then followed up with the sickle.

## Bisentō

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/bisento.webp" alt="A broad, heavy, curved blade with a thick spine on a stout shaft with a brass collar.">
<figcaption><em>Bisentō</em> (眉尖刀)</figcaption>
</figure>

The heavy halberd _bisentō_ (眉尖刀) came to Japan from China. It was far more massive than the _naginata_ (薙刀) and called for a different way of fighting. In the hands of a strong warrior, it was a formidable weapon.

## Bō

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/bo.webp" alt="A plain, straight red-oak staff tapering slightly toward each end, oiled and worn smooth.">
<figcaption><em>Bō</em> (棒)</figcaption>
</figure>

One of the simplest weapons was probably the staff. What is known today as the _bō_ (棒) is, strictly speaking, the _roku shaku bō_ (六尺棒), about 182 centimeters long. At first glance it looks harmless, but in the hands of a master this all-purpose weapon is deadly. It could thrust and strike, and it was effective against several opponents at once, which made it popular with the ninja.

## Bō Shuriken

The _bō shuriken_ (棒手裏剣) is an elongated _shuriken_ (手裏剣) shaped like a knife or an arrow.

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/bo-shuriken.webp" alt="Iron throwing spikes with square and round sections, each ground to a point at one end, a few with short thread tassels.">
<figcaption><em>Bō shuriken</em> (棒手裏剣)</figcaption>
</figure>

## Bō Ken

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/bokken.webp" alt="A solid red-oak practice sword in the shape of a sword, with a blunt, thick edge and a hand-worn grip.">
<figcaption><em>Bokken</em> (木剣)</figcaption>
</figure>

The _bō ken_, also _bokken_ (木剣) or _bokutō_ (木刀), is a practice sword made of heavy wood. It was usually even heavier than a sharp sword, so getting the _daishō_ (大小) at last came as a relief to a student.

## Bokken

Another name for the _bō ken_; also _bokutō_.

## Bokutō

Another name for the _bō ken_; also _bokken_.

## Chigiriki

A long stick with a chain, an iron weight at its end. Farmers originally developed the _chigiriki_ (乳切木) from the flail to defend themselves against attackers.

## Daisharin

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/daisharin.webp" alt="A stout, weathered wooden axle joining two large spoked wheels with iron rim bands.">
<figcaption><em>Daisharin</em> (大車輪)</figcaption>
</figure>

The _daisharin_ (大車輪) consisted of two wheels joined by an axle. The ninja liked to use it to knock larger groups of attackers off their feet, or as an improved staff.

## Daishō

_Daishō_ (<q>big-small</q>) is the name for the samurai’s pair of swords, the _katana_ (刀) and the _wakizashi_ (脇差). Strictly speaking, only samurai were allowed to carry two swords. Ninja didn’t care: as commoners, most of them weren’t allowed to carry weapons anyway.

## Daitō

The _daitō_ (大刀, <q>big sword</q>) is a sword with a particularly long blade, between 60 and 180 centimeters.

## Eiku

The boat oar _eiku_ is actually a weapon from Okinawa. A ninja disguised as a fisherman could defend himself well with it.

## Endoku Ken

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/endoku-ken.webp" alt="A four-pointed throwing star of thin iron with a hole in the center, a short burning stick pushed through it.">
<figcaption><em>Endoku ken</em></figcaption>
</figure>

Some throwing stars released poisonous smoke when lit. With these _endoku ken_, the ninja forced hidden enemies to show themselves.

## Fukiya

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/fukiya.webp" alt="A slender bamboo tube beside a bundle of small feathered darts with needle points.">
<figcaption><em>Fukiya</em> (吹き矢)</figcaption>
</figure>

The ninja liked the blowgun _fukiya_ (吹き矢) because it killed an enemy quietly. The darts were poisoned and stifled every scream. Disguised as a flute, a blowgun could be carried around freely.

## Fukumi Bari

The _fukumi bari_ (含み針) were small darts that the ninja spat into an attacker’s face.

## Futokoro Teppō

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/futokoro-teppo.webp" alt="A slender iron matchlock pistol with a wooden grip and a long, thin barrel, beside a plain wooden scabbard.">
<figcaption><em>Futokoro teppō</em> (懐鉄砲)</figcaption>
</figure>

The pocket pistol _futokoro teppō_ (懐鉄砲) was small and easy to hide, which made it popular with the ninja. Sometimes they fixed a bayonet to it.

## Hanbō

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/hanbo.webp" alt="A plain hardwood stick with a round section, worn smooth and slightly darkened at the grip.">
<figcaption><em>Hanbō</em> (半棒)</figcaption>
</figure>

Only 91 centimeters long and still an effective weapon: the _hanbō_ (半棒, <q>half staff</q>). Its proper name is _san shaku bō_ (三尺棒).

## Hankyū

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/hankyu.webp" alt="A short, asymmetric bow of laminated bamboo with a silk-wrapped grip.">
<figcaption><em>Hankyū</em> (半弓)</figcaption>
</figure>

The ninja’s bow, the _hankyū_ (半弓), was far shorter than the samurai bow. That made it easier to carry and to hide. The shorter range rarely mattered: ninja seldom had to hit distant targets on a battlefield, and their targets were usually close.

They often attached explosives or incendiaries to their arrows, all the more useful since almost every building in Japan was made of wood. Special arrows could also cut ropes or make a howling sound that struck terror into the enemy.

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/ya.webp" alt="A bundle of bamboo arrows, each with three feather fletchings and a forged iron head, the heads in varied shapes.">
<figcaption><em>Ya</em> (矢)</figcaption>
</figure>

## Hira Shuriken

The _hira shuriken_ (平手裏剣) is a flat, star-shaped throwing weapon.

## Hishi

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/hishi.webp" alt="A handful of hard, black, dried water chestnuts with natural horn-like spikes, scattered on bare earth.">
<figcaption><em>Hishi</em> (菱)</figcaption>
</figure>

Dried water chestnuts, _hishi_ (菱), served the same purpose as the _tetsubishi_ (鉄菱) but were easier to come by. They didn’t arouse suspicion either, because the plant was a common sight in farming country.

## Iga Dama

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/iga-dama.webp" alt="A small forged iron ball bristling with short spikes, dark with forge scale.">
<figcaption><em>Iga dama</em></figcaption>
</figure>

The _iga dama_ could be used like the _tetsubishi_, and could also be thrown at enemies. Ninja liked to hide them all over their houses so they could fight off attackers.

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/tetsu-mari.webp" alt="A hollow iron ball with long spikes radiating in all directions.">
<figcaption><em>Tetsu mari</em></figcaption>
</figure>

## Jirai

_Jirai_ (地雷) is another name for the _uzume bi_.

## Jō

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/jo.webp" alt="A plain hardwood staff with a round section, smooth and lightly worn.">
<figcaption><em>Jō</em> (杖)</figcaption>
</figure>

The walking staff _jō_ (杖) was a shortened form of the _roku shaku bō_. It reached to the hip or the stomach and could be used much like the long staff. Its proper name is _yon shaku bō_ (四尺棒).

## Jutte

The _jutte_ (十手) is a hand weapon with a hook that could catch a sword attack.

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/jutte.webp" alt="A forged iron rod with a single hook projecting from its base, a cord-wrapped grip, and a tassel.">
<figcaption><em>Jutte</em> (十手)</figcaption>
</figure>

## Kagi Yari

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/kagi-yari.webp" alt="A long wooden shaft with a straight, double-edged steel point and a curved steel hook branching just below it.">
<figcaption><em>Kagi yari</em> (鈎槍)</figcaption>
</figure>

The ninja of the Togakure-Ryū (戸隠流) and the Kumogakure-Ryū (雲隠流) often used the hook spear _kagi yari_ (鈎槍), also called _kama yari_ (鎌槍). With it they could get up into trees, over walls, or onto ships, and even move from tree to tree. Hidden on a raised post, a ninja could catch a victim with the hook and haul him up. The spear was also good for fishing, for breaking open doors, and for carrying luggage.

## Kama

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/kama.webp" alt="A curved, single-edged sickle blade set at a right angle into a short wooden handle.">
<figcaption><em>Kama</em> (鎌)</figcaption>
</figure>

The _kama_ (鎌) is a short-handled sickle. In Asia it was used to cut rice, grain, or sugar cane.

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/tatami-gama.webp" alt="A folding sickle with a wooden handle, the curved blade opened to a right angle, the hinge pin visible.">
<figcaption><em>Tatami gama</em></figcaption>
</figure>

## Kama Yari

Another name for the _kagi yari_.

## Kanzashi

The _kanzashi_ (簪) is a fork-shaped hairpin. The _kunoichi_ (くノ一) often used it as a weapon.

## Katana

The _katana_ is the samurai’s long sword, with a curved blade 61 to 76 centimeters long. It was made in an elaborate forging process from steel of different hardness. It is considered the best sword in the world.

## Kōgai

The _kōgai_ (笄) is a sword pin or a hairpin. The sword pin was actually a tool kept in the side of the _saya_ (鞘). But it could also be thrown or used to stab.

## Kongō

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/kongosho.webp" alt="A symmetrical bronze ritual implement with a dark patina, three prongs at each end curving inward to a point.">
<figcaption><em>Kongōsho</em> (金剛杵)</figcaption>
</figure>

The _kongō_ (金剛), Sanskrit _vajra_, is a scepter that symbolizes the diamond. Esoteric sects used it, and it also served as a striking weapon.

## Kubotan

The _kubotan_ (クボタン), also _yawara_ (柔), is a striking weapon shaped like a cylinder. It is good for holds and for putting pressure on joints. It is used much like the _kongō_.

## Kusari Fundō

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/kusari-fundo.webp" alt="A hand-forged iron chain with slightly uneven links and a tapered lead weight at each end.">
<figcaption><em>Kusari fundō</em> (鎖分銅)</figcaption>
</figure>

The chain _kusari fundō_ (鎖分銅), also _manriki gusari_ (万力鎖), was a useful weapon. It was easy to hide and could even catch sword blows, tie up opponents, choke them, or knock them off their feet.

## Kusarigama

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/kusarigama.webp" alt="A sickle blade on a wooden handle, a long iron chain running from the butt to a heavy iron weight.">
<figcaption><em>Kusarigama</em> (鎖鎌)</figcaption>
</figure>

The _kusarigama_ (鎖鎌) was a popular weapon. A chain one to two meters long hung from a rice sickle, with a weight at its end. When attacked, a ninja swung the weight around the attacker’s blade and forced him to lower his weapon. Then he struck with the sickle.

## Ku Shaku Bō

The _ku shaku bō_ (九尺棒) is an extra-long stick: 270 centimeters.

## Kyoketsu Shōge

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/kyoketsu-shoge.webp" alt="A straight, double-edged blade with a curved hook branching from its base, a long braided hair cord, and a large, flat iron ring at the far end.">
<figcaption><em>Kyoketsu shōge</em> (距跋渉毛)</figcaption>
</figure>

Probably the ninja’s most popular weapon was the _kyoketsu shōge_ (距跋渉毛): a knife with a hook, a rope 18 feet (about 5.49 meters) long tied to the handle, and a steel ring at the rope’s end. The rope was often made of women’s hair, because hair is particularly strong.

With this weapon, the ninja could control the opponent’s weapon, attack with the knife, block a sword stroke with the hook, or knock the enemy off his feet. The rope served for climbing and for tying up. Fireworks could also be fixed to the steel ring to blind the enemy.

## Kyū

Another name for the _yumi_ (弓).

## Mamukigama

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/mamuki-gama.webp" alt="A sickle on a short wooden handle, a chain running from its butt to a coiled pit viper.">
<figcaption><em>Mamukigama</em></figcaption>
</figure>

With the _mamukigama_, a poisonous animal such as a snake was tied to the end of the rope. The ninja hurled it at the enemy, which made the fight a great deal easier: the animal kept the opponent busy and might even kill him.

## Manriki Gusari

Also _kusari fundō_. The name _manriki gusari_ means <q>a chain with the power of 10,000</q>. The chain is 40 to 400 centimeters long with weights at the ends. Masaki Toshimitsu, a gatekeeper in Edo, developed it in the 18th century as a defensive weapon against swords.

## Metsubushi

A ninja who was discovered was usually outnumbered. To get away safely all the same, he blinded his enemies with powder, _metsubushi_ (目潰し). He kept it in eggshells or nutshells. The powder was made of chemicals, ash, sand, iron filings, pepper, or nettle hairs, and it could blind an enemy for a short time or destroy his eyesight for good.

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/metsubushi.webp" alt="Hollowed eggshells and small lacquered boxes full of gray-brown powder, some of it spilled.">
<figcaption><em>Metsubushi</em> (目潰し)</figcaption>
</figure>

## Nage Teppō

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/nage-teppo.webp" alt="A fist-sized sphere wrapped in paper and cord with a short fuse, its surface scorched and stained.">
<figcaption><em>Nage teppō</em></figcaption>
</figure>

These explosive grenades, _nage teppō_, were thrown at the enemy by hand or with slings. They worked on the same principle as a modern hand grenade.

## Naginata

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/naginata.webp" alt="A long, gently curved, single-edged blade on a lacquered oval shaft with a brass collar.">
<figcaption><em>Naginata</em> (薙刀)</figcaption>
</figure>

The glaive _naginata_ was a weapon of the _sōhei_ (僧兵) and the weapon of choice for samurai women. It came in different forms, such as the _nagamaki_ (長巻), which varied in the ratio of blade to shaft, in length, and in the thickness of the blade. It was especially effective against mounted warriors: as long as a spear and sharp enough to sever limbs. After World War II it was used mostly by women, in _naginatadō_ (薙刀道).

## Nagishi Shuriken

The _nagishi shuriken_, also _bō shuriken_, are arrow-shaped projectiles. A tassel at the end steadies their flight.

## Neko Te

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/neko-te.webp" alt="Pointed metal claws set in leather finger bands, worn on the fingertips of both hands.">
<figcaption><em>Neko te</em> (猫手)</figcaption>
</figure>

The _kunoichi_ sometimes wore a _neko te_ (猫手, <q>cat’s claw</q>) on each finger. With them they could tear terrible wounds into an attacker’s face.

## Ninja Tō

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/ninja-to.webp" alt="A short sword with a nearly straight blade, an oversized square iron guard, a plain black lacquered scabbard, and a long cord.">
<figcaption><em>Ninja tō</em> (忍者刀)</figcaption>
</figure>

The short sword _ninja tō_ (忍者刀), also _ninja ken_ or _shinobi katana_ (忍び刀), was an important tool in the ninja’s kit. Less pretty and less valuable than the samurai’s _tachi_ (太刀) or _katana_, it was useful. Its blade was much shorter than that of a _katana_ or _tachi_, so the ninja could move better and use it in close combat. Sometimes the blade was straight; sometimes the sword was just a shortened _katana_.

The _tsuba_ (鍔) carried no fine ornament. It was usually larger, made of plain steel, and often square, so it could serve as a climbing aid. The sword was worn at the side and only rarely, when climbing for instance, across the back or the stomach. Poisons, medicines, or chemicals were often hidden in the handle, and the _saya_ could be used as a blowgun or a breathing tube.

## Ninja Ken

Another name for the _ninja tō_; also _shinobi katana_.

## Nodachi

The _nodachi_ (野太刀) are extra-long samurai swords with blades of 100 to 180 centimeters. They were carried on the back or by a servant.

## Nuntebō

The _nuntebō_ is a fishing spear: a fisherman’s tool that could also be used as a weapon.

## Ōgama

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/ogama.webp" alt="A huge, heavy sickle blade on a long wooden shaft, a long iron chain with a weight running from its butt.">
<figcaption><em>Ōgama</em> (大鎌)</figcaption>
</figure>

The _ōgama_ (大鎌) was the battlefield version of the _kusarigama_. Its chain was strong enough to bring down horses, and the sickle pierced or cut through armor with ease.

## Ono

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/ono.webp" alt="A rectangular iron head with a straight cutting edge on a long, round wooden haft.">
<figcaption><em>Ono</em> (斧)</figcaption>
</figure>

The heavy battle ax _ono_ (斧) came to Japan from China after the fall of the Tang dynasty. It could smash castle gates, knock warriors off their horses, or shatter any attack made with a lighter weapon.

## Ōzutsu

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/ozutsu.webp" alt="A cannon built from wooden staves bound with rope and iron hoops, with a wide muzzle, resting in a timber cradle.">
<figcaption><em>Ōzutsu</em> (大筒)</figcaption>
</figure>

According to Togakure-Ryū tradition, its ninja used wooden cannons early on to win larger battles. The _ōzutsu_ (大筒) was effective against large cavalry units or castle gates.

## Ono Gama

The _ono gama_ combines a heavy battle ax with a _kama_. It was used by the _sōhei_ and the ninja.

## Roku Shaku Bō

A _shaku_ (尺) was 30.3 centimeters, and _roku_ means six. _Roku shaku bō_ is the exact name for the _bō_.

## San Shaku Bō

Three (_san_) _shaku_ of 30.3 centimeters each: the exact name for the _hanbō_.

## Senban Shaken

_Senban shaken_ is the name for a _shuriken_ with a hole in the middle.

## Shaken

A _shaken_ (車剣) is a _shuriken_ with several points.

## Shakuhachi

The _shakuhachi_ (尺八) is a bamboo flute with five finger holes. Its name gives its length: one _shaku_ and eight (_hachi_) _sun_, about 54.5 centimeters. The _komusō_ (虚無僧) played it. Ninja liked to disguise their blowguns as _shakuhachi_.

## Shakujō

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/shakujo.webp" alt="A wooden pilgrim staff topped with an ornate pierced metal finial carrying six loose rings.">
<figcaption><em>Shakujō</em> (錫杖)</figcaption>
</figure>

A ninja disguised as a monk liked to carry the priest’s staff _shakujō_ (錫杖). Rings hang from the end of the staff. With their jingling, the monk drove crawling creatures off the path, so that he would not step on an animal by accident and could keep the precept against killing.

## Shikomizue

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/shikomizue.webp" alt="A plain bamboo walking stick, partly drawn apart to reveal a slim, straight blade hidden inside.">
<figcaption><em>Shikomizue</em> (仕込み杖)</figcaption>
</figure>

On the road, a ninja couldn’t always carry a sword openly. The _shikomizue_ (仕込み杖) was a sword hidden in a stick. From the outside it looked like an ordinary staff, but that could change in a moment.

## Shinobi Shobō

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/shinbo.webp" alt="A short hexagonal stick about the width of a palm, with a metal ring on its side for one finger.">
<figcaption><em>Shinobi shobō</em></figcaption>
</figure>

The _shinobi shobō_ is a forerunner of modern brass knuckles and works the same way: the middle finger goes through a steel ring, and the rest of the weapon lies hidden in the fist.

## Shinobi Katana

Another name for the _ninja tō_; also _ninja ken_.

## Shinobi Kai

Another name for the _shinobi zue_ (忍び杖).

## Shinobi Tantō

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/shinobi-tanto.webp" alt="A short, straight dagger blade in a plain black lacquered mounting without ornament, beside its scabbard.">
<figcaption><em>Shinobi tantō</em> (忍び短刀)</figcaption>
</figure>

Like the samurai, the ninja used _tantō_, but theirs were usually disguised or hidden in their clothing. The _shinobi tantō_ (忍び短刀) was disguised as a short stick, much like the _shikomizue_.

## Shinobi Zue

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/shinobi-zue.webp" alt="A hollow wooden staff, a weighted iron chain spilling out of its open end.">
<figcaption><em>Shinobi zue</em> (忍び杖)</figcaption>
</figure>

Also _shinobi kai_. In medieval Japan, carrying a staff was perfectly ordinary; everyone took one along when traveling on foot. So the ninja liked to use staffs and sticks – only theirs were modified. Most were hollow, with knives, poisons, blinding powder, or weighted chains hidden inside.

When a ninja was attacked, he flipped open the cap of the staff in an instant. An iron ball on a chain flew out, and he used it to knock the enemy off his feet. Or he hurled poison, blinding powder, or other weapons at him. In no time, an ordinary walking staff became a spear, a poison sprayer, or a morning star.

## Shukō

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/shuko.webp" alt="A forged iron palm band with four tapered spikes, strapped with leather to a round ring around the wrist.">
<figcaption><em>Shukō</em> (手鉤)</figcaption>
</figure>

The _shukō_, also _tekagi_ (手鉤), is a terrible weapon. Pointed iron spikes sat on a kind of leather band around the arm and turned the ninja’s hand into a tiger’s claw. The spikes could tear terrible wounds into an attacker’s face. With the _shukō_ a ninja could also <q>take swords</q>, that is, stop a sword stroke, and climb walls or trees more easily. The counterpart for the feet was called _ashi kō_.

## Shuriken

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/shuriken.webp" alt="Flat, forge-blackened iron throwing stars with four to eight points and a hole in the center.">
<figcaption><em>Shuriken</em> (手裏剣)</figcaption>
</figure>

The best-known weapons of the ninja were the throwing blades: the star-shaped _hira shuriken_ and the dagger-shaped _bō shuriken_. Each form called for its own throwing technique. The blades could be poisoned and also used in close combat.

The typical throwing star of the Togakure-Ryū was the _senban shaken_. It had four points, looked like a diamond, and had a hole in the middle. Its edges were ground sharp. It was so thin and light that nine could always be carried together in an inside pocket.

## Sodezutsu

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/sodezutsu.webp" alt="A short, thick bronze hand cannon with a flared muzzle and a cord-wrapped grip.">
<figcaption><em>Sodezutsu</em> (袖筒)</figcaption>
</figure>

The _ōzutsu_’s little brother was the _sodezutsu_ (袖筒). This cannon could be fired from the hand and was a forerunner of the rifle.

## Tachi

The _tachi_ is an old long sword with a blade 61 to 76 centimeters long. It was usually more strongly curved at the hilt than the _katana_. It hung horizontally from the belt in front of the body, edge down; the _katana_, by contrast, was thrust through the belt edge up. At first the _tachi_ was a battle sword; later it was used mostly for ceremonial occasions.

## Tanegashima

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/tanegashima.webp" alt="A Japanese matchlock musket with an octagonal barrel, a brass serpentine, and a straight wooden stock.">
<figcaption><em>Tanegashima</em> (種子島)</figcaption>
</figure>

The _tanegashima_ (種子島), also _teppō_ (鉄砲), was an arquebus. The Portuguese brought the weapon to Japan, and the ninja were probably the first to see its value. Long before it was used in battle, ninja are said to have used it for assassinations.

## Tantō

The _tantō_ is a dagger with a blade less than 30 centimeters long. Many people from all walks of life carried it as a weapon of self-defense.

## Tekagi

Another name for the _shukō_.

## Tekkō

The _tekkō_ (鉄甲) is a set of brass knuckles, exactly like modern ones in appearance and function.

## Teppō

Another name for the _tanegashima_.

## Tessen

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/tessen.webp" alt="A closed folding fan with heavy, blackened iron ribs and solid outer plates, silk between the ribs.">
<figcaption><em>Tessen</em> (鉄扇)</figcaption>
</figure>

The war fan _tessen_ (鉄扇) was a popular weapon because at first glance it didn’t look like one. Its outer parts were made of steel; sometimes it was no longer a fan at all, just a piece of steel in the shape of one. With it you could inflict pain on an opponent’s joints or control him.

## Tetsubishi

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/tetsubishi.webp" alt="A group of rusted, forged iron caltrops, each with one of its four spikes pointing up.">
<figcaption><em>Tetsubishi</em> (鉄菱)</figcaption>
</figure>

A ninja on the run scattered a handful of these iron spikes on the ground. People in Japan wore straw sandals, so stepping on the spikes was extremely painful, and the pursuit was over. The brilliant thing about the _tetsubishi_: however they were thrown, one spike always pointed up. They also occur in nature, as _hishi_.

## Tetsubō

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/tetsubo.webp" alt="A wooden club thickening toward its striking end, iron spikes set in its upper third, an iron ring at the butt.">
<figcaption><em>Tetsubō</em> (鉄棒)</figcaption>
</figure>

Weapons like the _tetsubō_ (鉄棒) were probably used all over the world, and they are among the most primitive. Steel spikes fixed to a club made it more effective.

## Tōami

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/toami.webp" alt="A hand-knotted hemp net with lead weights around its edge, spread in a circle mid-throw.">
<figcaption><em>Tōami</em> (投網)</figcaption>
</figure>

The ninja used the net _tōami_ (投網) to defend themselves against many attackers at once. In fishing villages it was an everyday tool and drew little attention. It could also be set up as a trap in forests or corridors.

## Tonki

Another name for _shuriken_.

## Torinawa

The _torinawa_ (捕り縄) is a pointed shaft on a chain or cord, with a weight at the end.

## Tori no Ko

The _tori no ko_ (鳥の子) are firecrackers, brought over from China early on. They were used only to distract and confuse. Later, after black powder was introduced, explosives were made as well.

## Uzume Bi

The land mines _uzume bi_, also _jirai_, were buried just below the surface. An enemy who stepped on the box set off the firing mechanism and was killed or badly wounded by the explosion.

## Wakizashi

The _wakizashi_ (<q>companion of the belt</q>) is a short sword, a smaller likeness of the _katana_. Together with the _katana_ it formed the _daishō_.

## Wakizashi Teppō

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/wakizashi-teppo.webp" alt="A dagger with a wrapped wooden hilt from which a slender iron gun barrel extends instead of a blade, beside its empty wooden scabbard.">
<figcaption><em>Wakizashi teppō</em></figcaption>

The _wakizashi teppō_ hid a single-shot pistol inside a traditional short sword.

## Yon Shaku Bō

Four (_yon_) _shaku_ of 30.3 centimeters each: the exact name for the _jō_.

## Yari

<figure>
<img src="/images/weapons/yari.webp" alt="A straight, double-edged spearhead on a long lacquered shaft with a brass collar.">
<figcaption><em>Yari</em> (槍)</figcaption>
</figure>

Japanese spears, _yari_ (槍), were made for thrusting and were hardly ever thrown. Their small points slipped easily between the plates of samurai armor. The heads were usually two- or three-sided, and the spear was usually 200 to 350 centimeters long. Oda Nobunaga used _yari_ up to 640 centimeters long to protect his _teppō_ troops from cavalry.

The ninja often used the _yari_ to vault high over walls or people.

</figure>

## Yawara

Another name for the _kubotan_.

## Yumi

Also _kyū_. The bow is called _yumi_, the arrow _ya_ (矢). The Japanese bow was made from several layers of wood or bamboo and is about 220 centimeters long. It is one of the few bows in the world with an asymmetrical shape: the arrow rests in the lower third.
