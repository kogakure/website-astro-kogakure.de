## The Five Commandments

Whenever you are sad, unhappy, sick, wounded, or in despair, you should remember five commandments. That is the advice Hatsumi Masaaki gives.

### 1. Let Feelings Pass

Forget sadness, anger, envy, and hatred. Hatsumi advises letting them pass in a single breath, like smoke, and not giving in to such feelings.

### 2. Stay on the Path of Righteousness

Do not stray from the path of righteousness; live a life worthy of a human being. Sages have repeated this simple philosophy for many ages.

### 3. Let Go of Greed, Luxury, and Ego

Do not cling to greed, luxury, or your ego. If you learn the martial arts only for your own benefit and always lean on others because it is easier, these three desires will control you. That holds even for someone who has reached a high rank in the martial arts. The three desires distort the martial arts and humanity alike.

For Hatsumi, there are two paths to death: joy and sorrow. He means joy in the heavenly sense, not as pleasure. Whoever reaches this stage in life recognizes their true purpose.

### 4. Accept Suffering as a Test

Accept suffering, sorrow, and hatred as they are, and see them as a chance for a test from the Almighty. For Hatsumi, seeing everything as a blessing of nature is the noblest spirit in _ninpō_ (忍法). Anyone trying to get something done should expect that people obsessed with their own selfishness will sometimes portray their goodwill as bad.

### 5. Live Fully in Budō

Devote your time and mind fully to _budō_ (武道), and focus your mind deeply on _bujutsu_ (武術). Hatsumi recommends reading between the lines of the late Takamatsu’s story. There you will find the attitudes and methods that matter in ninjutsu training.
