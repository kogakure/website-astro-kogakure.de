## Ninja by Birth

In earlier times no one could learn the profession of the ninja. One was born a ninja and died a ninja. According to tradition, the toddler’s limbs were dislocated from their joints in early childhood, which later made it possible for the ninja to slip out of bonds. Even the youngest children of a ninja learned to stay aware of their surroundings. As they grew up, they were trained in the secret arts of their Ryū (流).

## Training as a Child

At five or six, their childhood games gradually turned into training. Through play they built up balance and dexterity. They balanced on thin poles that were hung higher and higher, and learned to turn, jump, sit, and bend up there without losing their balance.

From the age of nine, the focus shifted to conditioning and flexibility. The children practiced rolls, jumps, and movements similar to yoga. They learned to endure bitter cold and long fasts, to breathe imperceptibly, and to harden themselves against torture.

## Combat Training

As they grew older, they struck and kicked bundles of straw.

They learned the basic techniques of unarmed combat, and later the fundamentals of sword and staff fighting.

As teenagers, they learned to handle the special weapons of their Ryū. The curriculum included blade throwing, hidden weapons, and rope and chain techniques.

## Body Training

They practiced swimming and underwater tactics and learned to use nature to gather information or to hide. They spent countless hours in cramped spaces or hanging from trees, building patience, endurance, and resilience.

They learned to move silently, to run great distances, and to leap from tree to tree and from roof to roof. Ninja are said to have covered the 560 kilometers between Edo and Ōsaka in a few days. That sounds exaggerated, but Hatsumi insists that 110 kilometers a day was nothing unusual for a ninja. At that pace, the journey took just over five days.

Above all, ninja relied on explosive power, which let them react in a flash.

Later they studied acting and psychology. By observing themselves and others, they learned to understand the human psyche and to turn its weaknesses and limits to their advantage.

They also learned to make medicine and drugs, to break into buildings, to climb walls, to hang from ceilings, and to lie beneath floors.

And they learned to tie up an enemy, to escape successfully, and to draw maps.

## Mental Training

The development of the mind mattered as much as the body. Only together with the mind can the body achieve the extraordinary. One method of harnessing the mind’s powers was meditation and breathing. Breathing calmed and drove out distracting thoughts.

In a fight, composure, perseverance, and the conquest of fear were decisive. Only when free of thoughts of victory or defeat did ninja become capable of victory. Their will was resolute, clear, and calm, like a lake with moonlight reflected on its surface. Their movements were natural and relaxed. That is how they could defeat several attackers at once.

They learned _kuji-in_ (九字印), magical finger signs with which they sought to attune themselves to the course of things and so bring about change. There were 81 finger signs in all. Ninja drew on elements of _shugendō_ (修験道), _mikkyō_ (密教), and tantric teachings.

With _kuji-kiri_ (九字切り), ninja restored their confidence, gave themselves inner strength, or helped themselves in dangerous situations. Through _kuji-kiri_ they were said to sense hostility, sharpen their perception, and awaken the instincts of wild animals in humans.

Ninja also used _saimin jutsu_ (催眠術), the art of hypnosis. With the powers of their minds, they were said to be equal to any task.
